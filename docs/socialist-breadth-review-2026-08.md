# Socialist breadth and classification review — August 2026

This pass checks whether the catalog represents socialism as a plural political family rather than a synonym for central planning, Marxism-Leninism, or social democracy. It also separates catalog breadth from measurement: a historically important name is not added as a scored result until the question bank can distinguish it from adjacent traditions.

## Current coverage

The catalog contains fifteen labels in the `socialist` family: Democratic Socialist, Revolutionary State Socialist, Market Socialist, Marxist-Leninist, Council Communist, Syndicalist, Libertarian Socialism, Participism, Maoism, Trotskyism, Guild Socialism, Juche, Christian Socialism, Utopian Socialism, and Socialist / Marxist Feminism. Related traditions remain visible in other families when a different lineage is the better primary classifier, including Ecosocialism, Anarcho-Communism, Mutualism, Left-Wing Market Anarchism, Anarcha-Feminism, and modern Social Democracy.

That distribution follows the academic baseline. Socialism includes multiple institutional models—central planning, participatory planning, and market socialism—and multiple views of political agency and transition. It should not be defined simply as state control. Modern social democracy generally reforms a capitalist mixed economy, while democratic socialism seeks democratic social ownership and should not be collapsed into Marxism-Leninism.

## Corrections made in this pass

- Social Democrat no longer lists Marxism as a generic descriptive influence. Marxist social democracy is historically real, but contemporary social democracy is not inherently Marxist.
- Council Communism no longer lists Luxemburgism as a subtype. Rosa Luxemburg's theory overlaps with council-democratic and anti-bureaucratic themes but is not simply a branch of Dutch-German council communism.
- Christian Socialism now uses a `religious-socialist` subfamily rather than an undifferentiated socialist bucket.
- Utopian Socialism now uses an `early-socialist` subfamily, making its pre-Marxian historical position explicit.
- Anti-Imperialism is now a cross-cutting `anti-colonial` modifier rather than a socialist family member. Socialist anti-imperialism is important, but anti-imperialism is not exclusively socialist.
- The public label browser now displays family and subfamily counts, opens filtered groups automatically, and separately exposes related traditions that are not ordinary-quiz matches.
- Those related entries include focused traditions with dedicated topic questions and four source-backed socialist candidates without synthetic centroids: Marxian Socialism (Non-Leninist), Eurocommunism, Ujamaa / Nyerereism, and Arab Socialism.

## Additional traditions

### Ready for focused follow-up visibility

- **Socialist / Marxist Feminism** has an eight-item focused instrument measuring structural patriarchy, class and social reproduction, legal-equality reform, and anti-hierarchical strategy. It is a catalog specialist, not an ordinary global-axis result, and the combined name reflects the resolution the current follow-up can support.

### Visible catalog candidate with the highest measurement priority

- **Marxian Socialism (Non-Leninist)** is the largest remaining broad scoring gap. It is now searchable as a related tradition, but it is not a scored label. The scored catalog has Marxist-Leninist, Trotskyist, Maoist, and council-communist endpoints, but no broad result for a Marxian socialist who accepts class analysis and social ownership while rejecting Leninist party-state doctrine. A dedicated construct set should distinguish class analysis, democratic control, party organization, state transition, and reform or rupture before promotion.

### Searchable historical candidates requiring new constructs

- **Eurocommunism** is now searchable as a related tradition and requires questions about pluralist democracy, parliamentary strategy, autonomy from Soviet doctrine, and the role of communist parties before promotion.
- **Ujamaa / Nyerereism** is now searchable as a related tradition and requires questions about African socialism, rural communal organization, national development, self-reliance, and the historical use of state-directed villagization before promotion.
- **Arab Socialism** is now searchable as a related tradition and requires questions capable of separating socialism, pan-Arab nationalism, anti-colonial state-building, secularism, and one-party developmentalism before promotion.

### Better treated as subtypes until evidence supports separation

- **Fabianism** is a gradualist, research-and-reform current within democratic socialism and social democracy; it should initially be represented as a subtype or influence rather than a new general centroid.
- **Autonomist Marxism** is a distinctive current centered on worker autonomy, refusal, and struggles beyond traditional party and union structures, but the current global axes do not measure those distinctions directly.

## Promotion rule

No candidate above should receive a production centroid merely because its history is well documented. Promotion requires construct-matched questions, clear separation from adjacent catalog labels in software fixtures, and understandable result copy across normative, descriptive, and prescriptive layers. Until then, the honest product state is a visible but non-scored related tradition, not false numerical precision.
