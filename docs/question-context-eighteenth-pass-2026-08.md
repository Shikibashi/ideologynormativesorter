# Question-context eighteenth pass

This pass uses `2026-08-question-context-v23` and adds bespoke context/source
records to 21 active foundational questions in the authority, property, and
markets domains. The records clarify what each normative or prescriptive item
does and does not claim; they do not change prompts, response scales, axis
weights, or label assignments.

## Scope

| Items | Layer focus | Boundaries added |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `q0001`, `q0003`–`q0006` | Normative | Exit, territorial monopoly, emergency authority, political obligation, consent, elections, and inherited membership are related but non-identical legitimacy arguments. |
| `q0021`–`q0026` | Normative | Labor, title, conquest, privilege, rectification, personal possessions, independence, livelihood, and meaningful exit are kept distinct rather than treated as one labor or property theory. |
| `q0034`, `q0035`, `q0038`, `q0039` | Prescriptive/normative | Entry barriers, artificial scarcity, nationalization, public governance, contestability, reciprocal obligation, and property absolutism are separated as different reform and legitimacy questions. |
| `q0041`–`q0046` | Normative | Voluntary exchange, planning, plural ends, coercively restricted access, privilege removal, and the conditions for a moral defense of markets are not treated as universal efficiency claims. |

## Source basis

The context records use the existing public source catalog. The principal
references are the [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on authority](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/authority/),
[political obligation](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/political-obligation/),
[property](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/property/index.html),
[distributive justice](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/justice-distributive/index.html),
[markets](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/markets/),
[socialism](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2025/entries/socialism/), and
[liberalism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/liberalism/), together with
International IDEA’s [Emergency Powers](https://www.idea.int/publications/catalogue/emergency-powers)
guidance and the OECD’s [Guidelines on Corporate Governance of State-Owned
Enterprises](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2024/06/oecd-guidelines-on-corporate-governance-of-state-owned-enterprises-2024_68fa05cd/full-report.html).

These sources support construct definitions and distinctions. They do not
validate the quiz’s scoring geometry, establish causal effects, or determine a
respondent’s answer. Those claims still require cognitive interviews, pilot
data, test–retest evidence, internal-structure analysis, and fairness checks.
