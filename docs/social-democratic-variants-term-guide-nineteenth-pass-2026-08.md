# Social-democratic variants term-guide nineteenth pass

Date: 2026-08-11

## Scope

This pass reviewed four labels that still fell back to broad social-democratic
or welfare-state definitions: State-Capacity Egalitarian, Social Democrat,
Universal Basic Income Advocacy, and Social Investment State. It adds direct
term guides, layer summaries, and regression tests. It does not change
centroids, axis weights, question prompts, question selection, result
eligibility, or scoring behavior.

## Direct term guides

- **State-Capacity Egalitarian** now identifies an egalitarian social-
  democratic orientation that depends on capable and accountable public
  institutions and social provision. It is not authoritarian state socialism
  or generic egalitarianism without a state-capacity commitment.
- **Social Democrat** now identifies democratic collective action through a
  mixed economy, welfare provision, labor rights, progressive taxation, and
  gradual reform. It does not require abolishing capitalism or one socialist
  ownership model.
- **Universal Basic Income Advocacy** now uses the established basic-income
  criteria: an individual, periodic, unconditional cash payment to all,
  without a means test or work requirement. Proposals still vary over funding,
  level, eligibility, services, and other benefits.
- **Social Investment State** now identifies a welfare approach organized
  around building and maintaining human capabilities across the life course,
  including education, childcare, training, employment support, and buffers.
  It is not simply passive income maintenance or market deregulation.

Each guide is keyed to the stable catalog id and shown alone, so the specific
result is not displaced by a generic parent-family definition.

## Layer summaries

All four labels now have explicit normative, descriptive, and prescriptive
summaries:

- State-Capacity Egalitarian emphasizes material equality through capable,
  accountable public institutions and broad social provision.
- Social Democrat emphasizes democratic reform of a mixed economy through
  taxation, public services, unions, collective bargaining, and social
  insurance.
- Universal Basic Income Advocacy emphasizes an unconditional income floor,
  while leaving funding, level, eligibility, and relationships to existing
  benefits open to proposal-level disagreement.
- Social Investment State emphasizes capabilities, participation, and
  resilience across the life course rather than only compensatory transfers.

These distinctions separate equality goals, a political tradition, a specific
cash-transfer instrument, and a welfare-state policy paradigm. They are
editorial descriptions of the catalog profiles, not claims that every party or
proposal using one of these names has the same complete program.

## Source basis

- Ben Jackson, “Social Democracy,” _The Oxford Handbook of Political
  Ideologies_, on social democracy as democratic collective action extending
  freedom and equality into economic and social organization:
  https://www.history.ox.ac.uk/publication/631813/manual
- David Miller, “European Social Democracy,” _Oxford Bibliographies in
  Political Science_, on the movement’s gradualist or revisionist reform of
  capitalism through democratic institutions and its connection to welfare
  institutions:
  https://academic.oup.com/reference/62402/reference-article-abstract/555456429?login=false
- Basic Income Earth Network, “About basic income,” on periodic, cash,
  individual, universal, and unconditional payments:
  https://basicincome.org/about-basic-income/
- Anton Hemerijck, “Social investment as a policy paradigm,” _Journal of
  European Public Policy_ 25.6 (2018), on social investment as a welfare-state
  paradigm:
  https://doi.org/10.1080/13501763.2017.1401111
- Anton Hemerijck and Séverine Ronchi, “Recent developments: social
  investment reform in the twenty-first century,” _The Oxford Handbook of
  Welfare States_ (2021), on lifelong human-capital stocks, work-life flows,
  and inclusive buffers:
  https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordhb/9780198828389.013.7
- Francis Fukuyama, “What Is Governance?”, _Governance_ 26.3 (2013), for the
  state-capacity and public-goods context used in the State-Capacity
  Egalitarian synthesis:
  https://doi.org/10.1111/gove.12035

The catalog’s decision to treat these as separate result profiles is an
editorial taxonomy choice informed by the sources. The sources do not imply
one universally settled classification for every social-democratic party,
basic-income proposal, or social-investment reform.
