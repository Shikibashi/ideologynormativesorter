## Scope

## Layer decisions

Anarcho-Capitalism now has a descriptive summary that reports its expected institutional mechanism: private property, voluntary contracts, and competing providers of law, protection, and arbitration are expected to replace territorial state provision. It also records a central objection in the literature: consent-based, market-provided legal systems may struggle with equal access to law and effective remedies. This reports the doctrine's expectation and a scholarly criticism without treating either as a settled empirical result.

Bleeding-Heart Libertarianism now has a descriptive summary that reports the movement's characteristic empirical and institutional hope: markets, voluntary cooperation, and property rights can contribute to the well-being of vulnerable or least-advantaged people while preserving individual liberty. The summary preserves internal disagreement over how social justice bears on redistribution and institutional intervention rather than implying one settled program.

## Source basis

- Carmen E. Pavel, “The Rule of Law and the Limits of Anarchy,” _Legal Theory_ 27 (2021), identifies market anarchism with competing private security companies and explains the resulting questions about equality before the law, access to courts, and remedies: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/legal-theory/article/rule-of-law-and-the-limits-of-anarchy/A72C7270D96B21F42BA1950196CD4F4C
- “Law as a Public Good: The Economics of Anarchy,” _Economics & Philosophy_ 24 (2008), surveys the argument that law enforcement and property-rights protection can be supplied through private market relations: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/economics-and-philosophy/article/abs/law-as-a-public-good-the-economics-of-anarchy/AFDCFEFADE2C6C92CFF3CEE30E08531B
- The Bleeding Heart Libertarians founding statement describes the movement's shared attraction to market mechanisms, voluntary cooperation, property rights, individual liberty, and the contribution of those institutions to vulnerable people's ability to realize important human goods: https://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2011/03/bleeding-heart-libertarianism/
- Libertarianism.org's overview describes the movement's attempt to combine free markets with concern for social justice and the least well-off, while noting disagreements about the institutional causes of disadvantage and appropriate reforms: https://www.libertarianism.org/articles/libertarians-who-dismiss-social-justice-are-mistaken
