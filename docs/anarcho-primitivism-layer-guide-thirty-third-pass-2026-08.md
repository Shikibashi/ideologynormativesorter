# Anarcho-primitivism layer guide

Date: 2026-08-11

## Scope

This focused editorial pass adds the missing descriptive layer summary for
`anarcho-primitivism`. Its direct term definition and normative or practical
summaries were already specific, but the descriptive layer fell back to
generic anarchist text. The new copy identifies the anti-civilization causal
story while marking the label’s internal variation and contested
anthropological claims. No centroids, axis weights, question prompts, or
scoring behavior changed.

## Layer decision

**Anarcho-Primitivism** now describes the expectation that domestication,
agriculture, symbolic systems, division of labor, and industrial technology
intensify alienation, hierarchy, and ecological harm. It does not present
“primitive” societies as uniformly egalitarian or romanticize Indigenous
peoples: the current *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* entry explicitly
warns against that kind of projection, while scholarly criticism notes that
anti-civilization theories can understate the diversity of technologies and
social forms.

The summary is therefore a description of a characteristic critique, not a
claim that all anarcho-primitivists share one historical narrative or that
the narrative is settled anthropology.

## Source basis

- “Anarchism,” *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, revised December 2025,
  on anarcho-primitivism as an anti-civilizational form and on the need to
  avoid romantic projections about Indigenous societies:
  https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/anarchism/
- John Moore, “A Primitivist Primer,” a primary movement text summarizing the
  tendency’s critique of technology, division of labor, production,
  industrialism, and domestication:
  https://theanarchistlibrary.org/library/john-moore-a-primitivist-primer
- Matthew McDonald and others, “‘I don’t want your progress! It tries to kill
  … me!’ Decolonial encounters and the anarchist critique of civilization,”
  *Globalizations* (2022), on anarcho-primitivism’s anti-civilization theory
  and the criticism that it can neglect Indigenous science and convivial
  technologies:
  https://doi.org/10.1080/14747731.2022.2073657
