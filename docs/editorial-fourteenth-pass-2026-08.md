## q0147 — open standards

The earlier prompt attributed increased innovation to open standards and shared knowledge. The reviewed sources support a narrower claim about interoperability, switching or integration barriers, and diffusion in digital markets. They also identify risks: maintenance costs, obsolescence, technology entrenchment, and gatekeeper power. The prompt now tests only the conditional barrier-reduction mechanism.

Sources: [OECD — Stimulating digital innovation for growth and inclusiveness](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/stimulating-digital-innovation-for-growth-and-inclusiveness_5jlwqvhg3l31-en.html) and [OECD — Data Portability, Interoperability and Digital Platform Competition](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2021/10/data-portability-interoperability-and-competition_f09a402e/73a083a9-en.pdf).

## q0248 — official or preferred religion

The earlier prompt inferred that doctrinal disagreements become enforceable law wherever clerics have political power. The Pew analysis instead codes official or preferred religion and compares legal or practical preference and restrictions across countries. The revised item asks only the observed cross-national restriction pattern and explicitly preserves variation among ceremonial, preferential, restrictive, and hostile arrangements.

Source: [Pew Research Center — Many Countries Favor Specific Religions, Officially or Unofficially](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2017/10/03/many-countries-favor-specific-religions-officially-or-unofficially/), with the related [religious-restrictions analysis](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2019/07/15/a-closer-look-at-how-religious-restrictions-have-risen-around-the-world/).

## q0307 — CERCLA/Superfund enforcement

The earlier prompt claimed that clear pollution liability reduces harm when victims can identify and enforce claims. EPA sources directly support a narrower legal mechanism: identifying a potentially responsible party can trigger cleanup obligations, cost recovery, damages, or injunctive relief under CERCLA. The revised item does not generalize from that mechanism to pollution reduction under every liability regime.

Sources: [EPA — Superfund Liability](https://www.epa.gov/enforcement/superfund-liability) and [EPA — Superfund Enforcement](https://www.epa.gov/enforcement/superfund-enforcement).

## q0368 — DHS law-enforcement technologies

The earlier prompt generalized from a specific GAO review to federal agencies and detection technologies broadly. The revised item names the GAO/DHS scope and reports the measured findings: selected DHS law-enforcement agencies reported more than 20 types of detection, observation, and monitoring technologies in public spaces, and reviewed technology policies did not always address key privacy protections.

Source: [GAO-25-107302 — DHS Could Better Address Bias Risk and Enhance Privacy Protections for Technologies Used in Public](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-25-107302).
