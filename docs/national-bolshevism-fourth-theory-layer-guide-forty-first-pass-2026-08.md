# National Bolshevism and Fourth Political Theory layer guide — forty-first pass (2026-08)

## Scope

This pass adds only narrowly scoped descriptive-layer summaries for two labels whose direct term guides already identify substantial historical or author-specific variation. It changes consumer-facing layer copy and its tests only. No centroid, axis weight, question prompt, question selection, or scoring behavior changed.

## Layer decisions

- **National Bolshevism** now has a descriptive summary for the post-Soviet Limonov–Dugin current modeled by this catalog. It describes the current's expected political mechanism—a strong anti-liberal state combining national power with revolutionary-socialist mobilization—while retaining the historical-variation caveat. This is a scoped account of a current's characteristic expectation, not a claim that interwar German, Russian, and post-Soviet uses share one invariant program.
- **Fourth Political Theory** now has a descriptive summary for Dugin's stated project. It describes the expectation that civilizational pluralism and multipolar great spaces can replace liberal universalism and a unipolar order, while stating that the project is incomplete and that its claimed break with fascism is disputed. This does not treat Dugin's proposal or its geopolitical claims as settled political science.

The summaries use “expects” to preserve the distinction between a movement's characteristic belief and an independently established causal or predictive finding. The normative and prescriptive layers remain separate: they are not inferred from the descriptive additions.

## Source basis

- Jussi Backman, “Radical conservatism and the Heideggerian right: Heidegger, de Benoist, Dugin,” _Frontiers in Political Science_ 4 (2022), describes Fourth Political Theory as an alternative to liberalism, communism, and fascism, and discusses its communitarian, particularistic, traditionalist, and multipolar claims: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpos.2022.941799
- Jacob W. Kipp, “Aleksandr Dugin and the ideology of national revival: Geopolitics, Eurasianism and the conservative revolution,” _European Security_ 11, no. 3 (2002), traces Dugin's movement from the National Bolshevik Party toward Neo-Eurasianism: https://doi.org/10.1080/09662830208407539
- Daniele Bernardini, “Una ‘Fatalità tedesca’,” _Società e Storia_ 142 (2013), treats interwar National Bolshevism as a heterogeneous movement rather than one uniform doctrine: https://doi.org/10.3280/SS2013-142003
- Matthew Sharpe, “Old wine in a postmodern bottle: Aleksandr Dugin’s ‘Fourth political theory’ and Aurel Kolnai’s _War against the West_,” _Studies in East European Thought_ 78 (2026), reconstructs Dugin's claims and argues that the theory's novelty and claimed break with fascism require critical assessment: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11212-025-09703-3
- Stanford University's Freeman Spogli Institute summarizes the National Bolshevik Party's 1993 formation, its Limonov–Dugin leadership, and its German National Bolshevik influences: https://fsi.stanford.edu/docs/aleksandr-dugins-foundations-geopolitics
