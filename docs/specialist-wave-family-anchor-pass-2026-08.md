# Specialist wave family-anchor pass (2026-08)

This pass closes roster omissions in the experimental follow-up modules. The omissions mattered because a candidate module could otherwise compare a respondent with a narrow subtype while omitting the broader family anchor that the taxonomy says should remain available for separation.

## Roster additions

- Green morphology now includes **Bright Green Environmentalism** alongside ecomodernism and green capitalism. The candidate is deliberately close to those traditions on technology and market strategy; the module does not treat the term as a validated distinct identity.
- Socialist families now include **Democratic Socialism**, **Marxian Socialism**, and **Guild Socialism** alongside social democracy, market socialism, councilism, syndicalism, Maoism, and Trotskyism. The new profiles are coarse family anchors and cannot yet settle the boundaries between democratic socialism, non-Leninist Marxian socialism, guild socialism, or related traditions.
- Religious and national political projects now include **Religious Nationalism** and **Christian Reconstructionism**. Religious nationalism is an umbrella fusion of religious and national goals, not one theology; Christian Reconstructionism is a specific theonomic/Reformed current, not generic Christian conservatism.

The module remains opt-in and experimental. These additions improve candidate coverage and prevent an omitted-parent artifact; they do not promote any label into ordinary scoring or establish respondent validity. Source records and question contexts remain separate from measurement evidence.

## Research basis

The parent/variant distinctions follow the current catalog sources and the [Oxford account of religious nationalism](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/62239/chapter-abstract/550810397) as a fusion of religious and national identities or goals, the [SEP account of Marx](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/marx/) and its distinction between Marxian and utopian socialism, [Oxford scholarship on guild socialism](https://academic.oup.com/cje/article/47/1/21/6775929) as democratic worker control and social ownership, and recent [Cambridge discussion of bright-green environmentalism](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/earths-amphibious-transformation/environmentalisms-clashing/9CDA857A19D1C283E4630CB37CEDDD29) as technology- and innovation-oriented ecological politics.
