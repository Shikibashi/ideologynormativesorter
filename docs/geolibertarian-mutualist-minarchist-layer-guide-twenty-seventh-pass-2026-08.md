# Geolibertarian, Mutualist, and Minarchist layer-guide twenty-seventh pass

Date: 2026-08-11

## Scope

This pass reviewed three libertarian-family labels whose direct term guides
were already specific but whose descriptive layer still used generic fallback
copy. It adds explicit normative, descriptive, and prescriptive layer
summaries. It does not change centroids, axis weights, question prompts,
question selection, result eligibility, or scoring behavior.

## Layer summaries

- **Geolibertarianism** combines self-ownership and voluntary exchange with an
  equal claim to natural opportunities. It expects market exchange to coexist
  with socially generated land value and treats land rent as a candidate for
  public capture; its prescriptive profile favors private use of land paired
  with land-value or resource-rent collection. Variants differ over revenue
  use and state scope.
- **Mutualism** treats reciprocity, worker autonomy, equal exchange, and
  voluntary association as safeguards against both capitalist privilege and
  centralized state administration. It expects cooperative markets and
  mutual-credit institutions to widen access to productive resources and
  reduce exploitation, while implementations differ over property, exchange,
  and institutional scale. Its prescriptive profile favors mutual credit,
  cooperative exchange, use-based claims, and federated voluntary institutions.
- **Minarchism** treats individual rights in life, liberty, property, and
  contract as primary constraints on political authority while allowing a
  narrowly limited state to protect them. It expects public police, courts,
  and defense to protect against force, fraud, and rights violations more
  reliably than a wholly stateless arrangement. Its prescriptive profile is a
  minimal rights-protecting state, not anarcho-capitalist abolition of the
  state or broad social liberal government.

These summaries separate a land-rent correction to libertarian property
theory, a cooperative anarchist market tradition, and a rights-based defense
of a minimal state. They describe the catalog’s profiles rather than claiming
that every adherent or historical implementation shares one complete program.

## Source basis

- “Libertarianism,” *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, on self-ownership,
  left-libertarian egalitarian claims over the external world, and the link
  between land-value taxation and self-ownership:
  https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/libertarianism/
- Henry George, *Progress and Poverty*, Chapters 11 and 33, on the distinction
  between land rent and human improvements and the case for taxing land value:
  https://www.henrygeorge.org/pchp11.htm
  https://www.henrygeorge.org/pchp33.htm
- Javier Lloveras, Gary Warnaby, and Lee Quinn, “Mutualism as market practice,”
  *Marketing Theory* 20, no. 3 (2020), on mutualism, market exchange, mutual
  credit, and cooperative management of markets as a common:
  https://doi.org/10.1177/1470593119885172
- “Anarchism,” *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, Spring 2026 Edition, on
  the diversity of anarchist forms, libertarian and socialist currents, and
  mutualism’s place among decentralized anti-authoritarian approaches:
  https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2026/entries/anarchism/
- “Robert Nozick’s Political Philosophy,” *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*,
  on the minimal state’s protection of life, liberty, property, and contract
  and its contrast with individualist anarchism:
  https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nozick-political/
- “Criminal Justice,” Libertarianism.org, on the minarchist case for a
  government-administered police and court system as a limited rights-
  protection role:
  https://www.libertarianism.org/topics/criminal-justice

The catalog’s decision to keep these as separate result profiles is an
editorial taxonomy choice informed by the sources. The sources do not imply
one universally settled classification for every geolibertarian, mutualist,
or minarchist movement.
