# Religious-authority term-guide twenty-first pass

Date: 2026-08-11

## Scope

This pass reviewed four labels that still fell back to broad conservative or
religious-politics definitions: Christian Democrat, Theocratic Politics,
Integralism, and Fundamentalist Theocracy. It adds direct term guides, layer
summaries, and regression tests. It does not change centroids, axis weights,
question prompts, question selection, result eligibility, or scoring behavior.

## Direct term guides

- **Christian Democracy** now combines Christian social thought with
  democratic constitutionalism, subsidiarity, solidarity, social-market
  economics, and family or civil-society institutions. It is not equivalent
  to theocracy or one fixed church-state model.
- **Theocratic Politics** now identifies a political ordering in which
  religious authority or revealed law is the ultimate source of civil
  legitimacy and public law. It is not merely personal faith or every
  religiously inspired democratic movement.
- **Integralism** is specified here as Catholic integralism: a tradition that
  subordinates public order to a Catholic conception of the common good and
  divine moral authority, rejecting liberal church-state separation. It is
  distinct from Christian democracy and from every clerical-fascist alliance.
- **Fundamentalist Theocracy** combines theocratic rule with a strict or
  literal authoritative interpretation of sacred texts governing public
  institutions. It is more specific than theocracy and is not synonymous with
  religious conservatism.

Each guide is keyed to the stable catalog id and shown alone, so a specific
result is not displaced by a generic parent-family definition.

## Layer summaries

All four labels now have explicit normative, descriptive, and prescriptive
summaries:

- Christian Democrat emphasizes human dignity, solidarity, family and civil
  society, subsidiarity, constitutional democracy, and social-market
  institutions.
- Theocratic Politics emphasizes religious authority or divine law as the
  basis of public legitimacy, without fixing one religion or institutional
  form.
- Integralism emphasizes Catholicly ordered public authority and the common
  good rather than liberal church-state separation; clerical-fascist alliances
  are treated as one historical variant, not the whole tradition.
- Fundamentalist Theocracy emphasizes strict scriptural authority enforced
  through state institutions, with pluralist and secular alternatives more
  sharply constrained than in the generic theocracy category.

These distinctions separate a constitutional political tradition informed by
Christian social thought, a general source-of-authority category, a specific
Catholic tradition, and a scriptural-maximalist theocratic subtype. They are
editorial descriptions of the catalog profiles, not claims that every party,
church, movement, or regime using one of these names has the same complete
program.

## Source basis

- Paolo Pombeni, “Christian Democracy,” _The Oxford Handbook of Political
  Ideologies_, on Christian democracy’s relationship to constitutionalism,
  church-state relations, and Christian social doctrine:
  https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordhb/9780199585977.013.0003
- Carlo Invernizzi Accetti, “Subsidiarity,” in _What is Christian Democracy?_,
  on subsidiarity as a central Christian-democratic principle for distributing
  authority across local, national, and international levels:
  https://doi.org/10.1017/9781108368162.005
- “Theocracy,” _The Encyclopedia of Political Science_, on the placement of
  ultimate state authority in divine commission or religious law rather than
  treating the term as a synonym for personal belief:
  https://www.encyclopedia.com/social-sciences-and-law/political-science-and-government/political-science-terms-and-concepts/theocracy
- Kevin Vallier, “The Justice Argument Against Catholic Integralism,”
  _Political Studies_ (2024), on Catholic integralism’s claim that church law
  may direct state authority toward the church’s higher end:
  https://doi.org/10.1177/00323217221130169
- “Catholic Integralism and the Integralists,” _All the Kingdoms of the
  World_, on natural law, the common good, human dignity, church-state
  relations, religious liberty, and canon law in integralist thought:
  https://academic.oup.com/book/46743/chapter-abstract/413274573
- “Religious Fundamentalism: A Theoretical-Constitutional Analysis,” on
  fundamentalism as a religiously grounded political-control strategy rather
  than a synonym for all religious commitment:
  https://revistas.juridicas.unam.mx/index.php/cuestiones-constitucionales/article/view/18584

The catalog’s decision to treat these as separate result profiles is an
editorial taxonomy choice informed by the sources. The sources do not imply
one universally settled classification for every Christian-democratic,
theocratic, integralist, or fundamentalist movement.
