# Nationalism and religious-authority boundary pass — 2026-08

This pass reviews the boundaries among civic nationalism, ethnonationalism,
religious nationalism, theocracy, and fascism. It changes wording and one
question mapping; it does not claim that the current centroids have been
validated by respondents.

## Research findings

- Nationalism concerns the relationship between a nation or ethno-cultural
  community and political organization. Civic and ethnic nationalism are
  useful ideal types, but the distinction is not perfectly exclusive: civic
  criteria can be restrictive, and national membership can be framed as
  voluntary, involuntary, cultural, or political in different theories.
- Ethnonationalism makes ethnicity, descent, cultural ties, or inherited
  collective identity central to the nation. It does not entail one legal
  membership rule, primordialist theory, racial science, territorial outcome,
  or level of coercion. The catalog therefore no longer treats primordialism
  as the descriptive theory of the entire label.
- Religious nationalism is the fusion of religious and national identities or
  goals. It can operate through cultural identity, political membership,
  public-law influence, state-building, or religious authority; it is not
  automatically a theocracy and is not equivalent to personal religiosity.
- Fascism is narrower than generic authoritarianism or nationalism. The
  catalog retains palingenetic national rebirth, anti-liberal mass politics,
  hierarchy, and coercive authority as the bounded fascist prototype.

## Implementation

- Civic Nationalist wording now says “primarily” and acknowledges that civic
  criteria can restrict access.
- Ethnonationalist metadata no longer treats primordialism as the whole
  label’s descriptive philosophy; it names primordialist nationalism as one
  variant and explicitly bounds the ordinary prototype.
- Religious Nationalism now describes a range of identity, membership,
  authority, and state-building relationships rather than implying one
  theocratic program.
- Fundamentalist Theocracy wording now says “often restricting” pluralism
  rather than asserting that every case suppresses all alternatives.
- `q0415` now measures civic membership versus ancestry only. Religious
  authority remains in the separate religion-and-law items.
- `q0405` now tests whether religious tradition may inform public law without
  treating inherited heritage as the operative construct.
- Fascism has an additional public historical reference from the United States
  Holocaust Memorial Museum, kept separate from scholarly and primary-text
  sources.
