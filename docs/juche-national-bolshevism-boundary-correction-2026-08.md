# Juche and National Bolshevism boundary correction — 2026-08

This pass revises two provisional specialist records without changing their
centroids, specialist status, or ordinary scoring eligibility.

## Juche

The catalog now describes Juche as the DPRK/Kimist state ideology of political
independence and state-directed self-reliance, with military self-defense and
leader-centered party-state authority. It no longer calls the ideology
“economic self-sufficiency,” which could be read as a claim of literal
autarky. The caution note also records that the ideology developed from
Marxist-Leninist materials and changed through Kimist succession politics.

Sources:

- Thomas Stock, “The Making of Chuch’e Philosophy,” _North Korean Ideology: A
  Cold War History_, Oxford University Press:
  [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/60614/chapter-abstract/524677518)
- Hazel Smith, “‘Socialism in our own style’,” _North Korea: Politics,
  Economy and Society_, Cambridge University Press:
  [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/north-korea/socialism-in-our-own-style/3CC34A21BCDF3BEB1EDA6FB9449CA6F8)

## National Bolshevism

The catalog now calls the tradition a historically variable set of attempts
and explicitly identifies the post-Soviet Limonov–Dugin milieu as the
reference case modeled by this provisional entry. It cautions that interwar
German National Bolshevism, National Communism, and other red-brown currents
do not share one settled program and should not be collapsed into orthodox
Marxism-Leninism or generic nationalism.

The boundary is supported by [Historical Legacies and the Size of the
Red-Brown Vote in Post-Communist Politics](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0967067X09000531),
which treats the term as historically situated and variable across
post-communist settings.
