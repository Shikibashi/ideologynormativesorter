# Descriptive evidence triangulation pass — 2026-08

The active core bank contains 58 descriptive items. Before this pass, 40
empirical items had one claim-specific source even though the normative and
prescriptive layers generally carried multiple background sources. The
`2026-08-descriptive-evidence-v5` overlay adds one additional academic or
institutional source to each of those 40 items without changing prompts,
weights, evidence notes, or answer semantics.

The first source remains the original claim record. The added source is used
to triangulate the mechanism, boundary, or institutional setting; it is not
treated as a second independent estimate of the effect and does not validate
the axis centroid. Several additions deliberately pair an empirical study
with a conceptual or governance source, because a claim about observed
performance should not be generalized beyond its population, period,
comparison, or outcome.

Examples include polycentric police provision and common-pool monitoring,
state-owned-enterprise accountability, regulatory capture, SNAP take-up,
housing supply, heterogeneous monetary transmission, patent litigation,
police performance measures, forfeiture incentives, immigration lobbying,
interethnic civic networks, environmental compliance, Afghanistan
reconstruction, political connections in procurement, deliberative knowledge,
democratic backsliding, biometric-risk governance, expert evidence, and
formal/informal norm change.

The pass intentionally does not promote descriptive agreement into a policy
recommendation. The question UI continues to show context and sources as
interpretive aids, and respondent confidence remains separate from the
source count. Respondent validation, replication, and psychometric testing
are still required before treating any axis as a validated scale.

Research basis includes the [Oxford treatment of polycentric metropolitan
governance](https://academic.oup.com/book/35300/chapter/299919173),
[Cambridge research on formal and informal institutions](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1744137424000249),
[BIS research on heterogeneous monetary effects](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1124.htm),
[NIJ work on policing performance measures](https://nij.ojp.gov/library/publications/revisiting-measuring-what-matters-developing-suite-standardized-performance),
[SAGE research on forfeiture incentives](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0047235211000316),
and [Oxford research on durable social-norm change](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780198899952.003.0004).
