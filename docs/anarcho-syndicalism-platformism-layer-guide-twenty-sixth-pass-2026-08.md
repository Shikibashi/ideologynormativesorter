## Scope

This pass reviewed two adjacent anarchist-organization labels whose direct
term guides were already specific but whose descriptive layer still used a
generic missing-summary fallback. It adds explicit normative, descriptive, and
prescriptive layer copy. It does not change centroids, axis weights, question

## Layer summaries

- **Anarcho-Syndicalism** treats worker solidarity, self-management, and
  freedom from capitalist and state domination as central goods. It expects
  industrial unions, direct action, and federated worker organization to build
  class power and provide a basis for social coordination, while historical
  movements differed over reform, revolution, and union structure. Its
  prescriptive profile favors replacing capitalism and the state with
  federated worker organizations rather than relying on electoral politics.
- **Platformism** treats anarchist-communist emancipation and coordinated
  collective action as compatible with anti-authoritarian politics. It expects
  a shared political program, tactical coordination, collective responsibility,
  and federal organization to make anarchist movements more coherent and
  effective. Its prescriptive profile favors a unified but decentralized
  organization rather than an unstructured federation or a centralized party.

These summaries distinguish a labor-centered strategy for anarchist
transformation from a proposal about how anarchists should organize
themselves. They describe the catalog’s profiles rather than claiming that
every historical movement using either label had one complete or uniform
program.

## Source basis

- “Anarchism,” *Routledge Encyclopedia of Modernism*, on anarcho-syndicalism’s
  focus on trade unions as agents of social transformation and foundations of
  post-state social organization:
  https://www.rem.routledge.com/articles/anarchism
- Hobart A. Spalding, “Anarchism and Anarchosyndicalism,” *Encyclopedia of
  Latin American History and Culture*, on direct action, strikes, boycotts,
  federated unions, and the variation of anarchosyndicalist practice over time:
  https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/anarchism-and-anarchosyndicalism
- Dielo Truda, *Organisational Platform of the Libertarian Communists* (1926),
  on the proposed anarchist organization and its principles of theoretical
  unity, tactical unity, collective responsibility, and federalism:
  https://theanarchistlibrary.org/library/dielo-truda-workers-cause-organisational-platform-of-the-libertarian-communists

The catalog’s decision to keep Anarcho-Syndicalism and Platformism as
separate profiles is an editorial taxonomy choice informed by these sources.
The sources support their organizational emphases but do not imply that every
anarchist-syndicalist or platformist group shares one identical strategy.
