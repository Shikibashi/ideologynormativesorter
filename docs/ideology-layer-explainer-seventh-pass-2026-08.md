# Ideology layer-explainer revision

Date: 2026-08-11

## Scope

This follow-up reviewed consumer-facing normative, descriptive, and prescriptive summaries against the catalog metadata that already defines each label. It does not alter label centroids, scoring roles, families, or subfamilies.

The review found 13 cases where the label description already stated a clear layer-specific commitment but the interface either showed a generic missing-summary fallback or reused an influence note from the wrong layer. Explicit summaries now cover:

- Ecomodernism: foundational compatibility of human flourishing and ecological protection.
- Anarcho-capitalism: voluntary and competitive provision of law, protection, and arbitration.
- Market socialism: the coordinating role retained for prices and competition under social or cooperative ownership.
- Christian democracy: subsidiarity, social-market institutions, welfare and labor protections, and civil society.
- Republicanism: civic self-government and institutional checks on arbitrary power.
- Distributism: dispersed ownership among families, small firms, guilds, cooperatives, and local associations.
- World federalism: democratic world-level federal institutions and enforceable international law.
- Radical democracy: participation and institutional contestability beyond periodic elections.
- Christian socialism: social ownership, economic democracy, and structural limits on exploitation.
- Green capitalism: its ecological value commitment and its market-centered transition instruments are now separated by layer.
- State corporatism: occupational and sectoral bodies organized under strong state direction.
- Liberal feminism: equal-rights and equal-opportunity reform within liberal-democratic institutions.

## Editorial boundary

These summaries restate existing catalog descriptions instead of inferring doctrine from synthetic centroid coordinates. Contested role changes for monarchy and social conservatism remain unchanged because this pass did not establish a safe taxonomy reclassification.
