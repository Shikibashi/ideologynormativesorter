# Municipalist, anarcha-feminist, and voluntaryist layer guide

Date: 2026-08-11

## Scope

This focused editorial pass adds the missing descriptive layer summaries for
`libertarian-municipalism`, `anarcha-feminism`, and `voluntaryism`. Their
direct term definitions and normative or practical summaries were already
specific, but their descriptive layers fell back to generic related-tradition
text. The new copy keeps three distinct claims separate: local democratic
coordination, intertwined gendered domination, and the effects of compulsory
state funding. No centroids, axis weights, question prompts, or scoring
behavior changed.

## Layer decisions

- **Libertarian Municipalism** now describes the expectation that face-to-face
  assemblies and confederated municipalities can make power more accountable
  than centralized administration. It preserves the difference between
  reformist municipal participation within states and Bookchin’s more radical
  stateless communalism.
- **Anarcha-Feminism** now describes the expectation that patriarchy, gendered
  labor, sexual regulation, and other coercive hierarchies reinforce one
  another. It states that currents differ over causal links rather than
  presenting one universal feminist theory.
- **Voluntaryism** now describes the expectation that compulsory taxation and
  state direction produce intrusion, dependency, or distorted incentives,
  while voluntary support can sustain public functions or a limited state. It
  retains the distinction between Auberon Herbert’s voluntary-funded state
  and later anti-state currents.

These are descriptive accounts of characteristic arguments, not endorsements
or empirical claims about every municipality, feminist movement, or voluntary
association.

## Source basis

- Murray Bookchin, “Libertarian Municipalism: An Overview,” on local popular
  assemblies, mandated and recallable delegates, and confederation:
  https://www.theanarchistlibrary.org/library/murray-bookchin-libertarian-municipalism-an-overview
- Murray Bookchin, “Municipalization,” on decentralized confederal popular
  power as an alternative to centralized bureaucratic administration:
  https://theanarchistlibrary.org/library/murray-bookchin-municipalization
- Donna M. Kowal, “Anarcha-Feminism,” in *The Palgrave Handbook of Anarchism*
  (2019), on sexual double standards, gendered labor, women’s autonomy, and
  anarcha-feminism as a distinct anarchist tradition:
  https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-75620-2_15
- Auberon Herbert, “The Principles of Voluntaryism and Free Life” (1897), on
  voluntary taxation, state compulsion, and the voluntarily funded state:
  https://www.panarchy.org/herbert/voluntaryism.html
- Eric Mack, “Voluntaryism: The Political Thought of Auberon Herbert,” on
  Herbert’s voluntary-state position and its distinction from later currents:
  https://mises.org/journal-libertarian-studies/voluntaryism-political-thought-auberon-herbert
