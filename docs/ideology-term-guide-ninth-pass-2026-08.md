# Ideology term-guide ninth pass

Date: 2026-08-11

## Scope

This post-release pass reviewed labels whose names identify a specific form of worker or anarchist organization but whose interface term guides were absent or could fall back to a broader parent family. It changes explanatory copy only; it does not change centroids, scoring, question selection, or taxonomy placement.

## Corrections

- **Council Communism** now identifies workers’ councils as the intended organs of political power and control of production, while distinguishing the tradition from parliamentarism and vanguard-party rule.
- **Revolutionary Syndicalism** now identifies worker-run unions and direct action as both a means of struggle and the proposed institutional basis of later worker control.
- **Anarcho-Syndicalism** now states the combination directly: anarchist anti-state aims pursued through federated labor organization and direct action.
- **Platformism** now identifies the 1926 *Organisational Platform* and its four organizational principles: theoretical unity, tactical unity, collective responsibility, and federalism.

Each label is configured to show its specific guide without crowding it with a generic Marxism, socialism, or anarchism definition. These guides describe the catalog’s intended use of contested historical terms; they do not claim that every adherent or organization used identical doctrine.

## Source basis

- Saeed Rahnema, “The Inconceivability of Real ‘Workers’ Control’ under Capitalism,” *International Labor and Working-Class History* 103 (2023), especially the discussion of Pannekoek, council communism, workers’ councils, and opposition to vanguard-party substitution: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0147547922000308
- Sean Scalmer, “Direct Action: The Invention of a Transnational Concept,” *International Review of Social History* 68, no. 3 (2023), on the strike, boycott, sabotage, and related practices at the core of syndicalist direct action: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0020859023000391
- “Anarchism,” *Routledge Encyclopedia of Modernism*, on anarcho-syndicalism’s focus on trade unions as agents of transformation and foundations of post-state social organization: https://www.rem.routledge.com/articles/anarchism
- Dielo Truda, *Organisational Platform of the Libertarian Communists* (1926), organizational section on theoretical unity, tactical unity, collective responsibility, and federalism: https://theanarchistlibrary.org/library/dielo-truda-workers-cause-organisational-platform-of-the-libertarian-communists
