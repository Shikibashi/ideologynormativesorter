## q0033 — property categories

The previous prompt bundled personal possession, productive assets, land rents, and state-created privileges. The revised item tests one distinction: personal possessions versus ownership or control of productive assets. The SEP property entry treats property as rules governing access and control, and distinguishes land, natural resources, means of production, manufactured goods, and different private, common, and collective arrangements.

Source: [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Property and Ownership](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/property/index.html).

## q0036 — worker cooperatives

The previous prompt bundled cooperatives, small firms, mutual aid, and independent work. The revised item isolates worker-owned and worker-governed cooperatives. The ILO describes worker cooperatives as member-worker-owner enterprises with democratic governance; those organizational forms should not be treated as interchangeable.

Sources: [ILO — Worker cooperatives](https://www.ilo.org/worker-cooperatives) and [ILO — Cooperatives and the Fundamental Principles and Rights at Work](https://www.ilo.org/publications/cooperatives-and-fundamental-principles-and-rights-work-cooperatives-and-0).

## q0193 — incapacitation threshold

The previous prompt combined restitution, prevention, and incapacitation. The revised item tests only whether incapacitation requires necessity to prevent serious harm. Restitution, rehabilitation, deterrence, and prevention remain distinct justice aims.

Sources: [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Legal Punishment](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/legal-punishment/) and [United Nations — International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights](https://2covenants.ohchr.org/About-ICCPR.html).

## q0354 — regulator accountability

The previous prompt bundled transparency, appeals, sunset review, and competitive alternatives. The revised item tests a coherent procedural-accountability construct: reasons for consequential decisions and a meaningful avenue to appeal. OECD research identifies accountability and transparency as core elements of regulator governance and treats transparent processes and appeal possibilities as important enforcement safeguards.

Sources: [OECD — Governance of Regulators' Practices](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/governance-of-regulators-practices_9789264255388-en.html) and [OECD — Regulatory Enforcement and Inspections Toolkit](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-regulatory-enforcement-and-inspections-toolkit_9789264303959-en/full-report/component-11.html).

## q0375 — government data access

The previous prompt bundled warrants, minimization, and adversarial oversight. The revised item tests case-specific authorization under clear legal rules. Human Rights Committee General Comment No. 16 explains that privacy interference must have a legal basis, defined circumstances, designated authorization, case-by-case control, and complaint/remedy channels; the item does not require one universal warrant procedure.

Sources: [UN Human Rights Committee — General Comment No. 16 (Article 17)](https://docstore.ohchr.org/SelfServices/FilesHandler.ashx?enc=NDHbUvPo0H0e6ReM%2BeoASfvbY3aH6JttLpsRvkmL87wD80W4xnAiRpmKA6ZLwTp9xzCLNUYwTBP35uH1Fw6yqQ%3D%3D) and [United Nations — International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights](https://2covenants.ohchr.org/About-ICCPR.html).

## q0376 — public-algorithm contestability

The previous prompt bundled benefits, policing, and immigration. The revised item tests contestability for consequential public algorithms: affected people should be able to inspect an audit of use and challenge the resulting decision. The context note preserves the distinction between audit access and appeal and warns that safeguards depend on the decision context, legal authority, confidentiality, and error costs.

Sources: [NIST — AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework), [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Ethics of Artificial Intelligence and Robotics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-ai/), and [United Nations — International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights](https://2covenants.ohchr.org/About-ICCPR.html).
