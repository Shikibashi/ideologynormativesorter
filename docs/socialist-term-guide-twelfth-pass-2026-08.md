# Socialist term-guide twelfth pass

Date: 2026-08-11

## Scope

This pass reviewed eight socialist labels whose consumer term guides were absent or fell back to generic definitions of socialism or Marxism. It adds doctrine-specific term guides and practical-layer summaries, and corrects narrowly inconsistent catalog metadata. It does not change centroids, axes, questions, weights, label eligibility, or scoring behavior.

## Direct term guides

- **Revolutionary State Socialist** is now identified as a catalog umbrella for centralized state-socialist transition strategies, not as one historical school or every form of revolutionary socialism.
- **Marxism-Leninism** now identifies the Soviet tradition codified under Stalin around vanguard-party leadership, centralized revolutionary state power, and planned social ownership, while acknowledging variation among later national forms.
- **Libertarian Socialism** now identifies the anti-capitalist and anti-authoritarian family centered on worker self-management and federation, and distinguishes it from right-libertarian market doctrines.
- **Maoism** now identifies mass-line politics, peasant mobilization, protracted people’s war, and continuing revolution against bureaucratic or capitalist restoration.
- **Trotskyism** now identifies permanent and international revolution, opposition to socialism in one country, and criticism of Soviet bureaucratic degeneration.
- **Guild Socialism** now identifies the British proposal for public ownership administered through democratic worker guilds rather than private capital or direct state bureaucracy.
- **Christian Socialism** now identifies a diverse family applying Christian social ethics to industrial capitalism without presenting one ownership model or reform strategy as universal.
- **Utopian Socialism** now identifies a retrospective grouping of the distinct Saint-Simonian, Fourierist, and Owenite projects rather than one doctrine.

Each guide is keyed to the stable label id and shown alone, so the interface does not substitute a generic parent-family definition for the named tradition.

## Metadata corrections

- Titoism is no longer listed as a Marxism-Leninism subtype. The existing usage note still records its claimed continuity and heterodox break with Soviet orthodoxy.
- Maoism and Trotskyism now appear in their own philosophy and practical-strategy metadata instead of being represented only by their Marxist and Leninist parents.
- Guild Socialism no longer lists Distributism as one of its philosophies. Distributism favors dispersed private productive property; Cole-style guild socialism proposed public ownership administered through worker guilds.
- Christian Socialism no longer lists Liberation Theology and the Social Gospel as subtheories. They remain named as related, overlapping movements, while Christian Democracy is distinguished from socialism.
- The Christian Socialist description and practical summary now reflect historical disagreement over cooperatives, regulation, welfare, social ownership, markets, and reform strategy.
- Utopian Socialism is now described as a later retrospective category rather than a unified self-description.

## Layer policy

The practical summaries describe the characteristic institutions or strategies that distinguish these labels. No empirical-belief summary was added where the tradition does not supply one common, testable descriptive doctrine.

## Source basis

- “Socialism,” *Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, on socialism as social rather than merely state control and on disagreement among socialist traditions over markets, planning, and institutions: https://iep.utm.edu/socialis/
- Vladimir Lenin, *What Is to Be Done?* (1902), a primary statement of revolutionary organization and political leadership: https://www.marxists.org/archive/lenin/works/1901/witbd/
- Joseph Stalin, *The Foundations of Leninism* (1924), especially the chapter on the party, a primary source for the later Soviet codification: https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/stalin/works/1924/foundations-leninism/ch08.htm
- Ana Antić, “The Pedagogy of Workers’ Self-Management: Terror, Therapy, and Reform Communism in Yugoslavia after the Tito-Stalin Split,” *Journal of Social History* 50, no. 1 (2016), on the 1948 break with Soviet orthodoxy and the subsequent Yugoslav model of self-management and nonalignment: https://academic.oup.com/jsh/article/50/1/179/2413022
- David Goodway, “Anarchism and libertarian socialism in Britain,” in *Anarchist Seeds Beneath the Snow* (Liverpool University Press, 2006), on the historically overlapping but nonidentical anarchist and libertarian-socialist currents: https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/927233C4101A674B99ED61CE1723D5AD/9781781386118c2_p15-34_CBO.pdf/anarchism-and-libertarian-socialism-in-britain-william-morris-and-the-background-1880-1920.pdf
- Elizabeth J. Perry, “Debating Maoism in Contemporary China,” *Made in China Journal* 6, no. 3 (2021), on Maoism’s combination of hierarchical party organization and mass or peasant mobilization: https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/68C3DB4A42D2363DB72C7116929FDFD5/S1557466021030734a.pdf/debating_maoism_in_contemporary_china_reflections_on_benjamin_i_schwartz_chinese_communism_and_the_rise_of_mao.pdf
- Leon Trotsky, *The Permanent Revolution* (1930), a primary statement of uninterrupted and international revolution: https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/1931/tpr/prre.htm
- Geoffrey M. Hodgson, “The institutional impossibility of guild socialism,” *Cambridge Journal of Economics* 47, no. 1 (2023), on public ownership, worker guild administration, and the distinction from worker-owned cooperatives: https://academic.oup.com/cje/article/47/1/21/6775929
- “Christian Socialism,” *International Encyclopedia of the Social Sciences*, on the tradition’s contextual diversity, reformist and cooperative currents, and distinction from Christian Democracy: https://www.encyclopedia.com/philosophy-and-religion/christianity/protestant-denominations/christian-socialism
- David Leopold, “Marx, Engels, and Non-Foundational Arguments Against Utopian Socialism,” in *Reassessing Marx’s Social and Political Philosophy* (Routledge, 2018), on the later grouping of Saint-Simon, Fourier, and Owen as the founding utopian socialists: https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/edit/10.4324/9781315398068-4/marx-engels-non-foundational-arguments-utopian-socialism-david-leopold
