# Community, republican, bioregional, and political-Islam term-guide twenty-fifth pass

Date: 2026-08-11

## Scope

This pass reviewed four labels that still used broad pattern-based help rather
than stable label-specific definitions: Communitarianism, Republicanism,
Bioregionalism, and Political Islam. It adds direct term guides, layer
summaries, and regression tests. It does not change centroids, axis weights,
question prompts, question selection, result eligibility, or scoring behavior.

## Direct term guides

- **Communitarianism** now identifies a family of views in which identities,
  moral judgment, and obligations are formed through constitutive communities,
  traditions, and social relations. It gives common goods weight alongside
  individual rights without prescribing socialism or one anti-liberal state.
- **Republicanism** now identifies the political-theory tradition of civic
  self-government and freedom as non-domination through rule of law and
  accountable institutions. It is not a contemporary party label or one
  constitutional template.
- **Bioregionalism** now identifies place-based environmental politics organized
  around ecological regions, often watersheds and connected human-environment
  relationships. It is not simply localism and does not choose one economic
  ownership model.
- **Political Islam** now identifies a broad and contested family of projects
  that make Islamic principles a reference for public authority, law, or
  political identity. It spans democratic, constitutional, electoral, activist,
  state-building, and violent currents without making Islam or Muslim civic
  participation itself political Islam.

Each guide is keyed to the stable catalog id and shown alone, so a specific
result is not displaced by a generic parent-family definition.

## Layer summaries

All four labels now have explicit normative, descriptive, and prescriptive
summaries:

- Communitarianism treats shared community, social membership, tradition, and
  common goods as morally important; it expects context and relationships to
  shape judgment and favors civic and community-supporting institutions without
  fixing one state or economic program.
- Republicanism treats equal civic standing, self-government, rule of law, and
  freedom from arbitrary power as central goods; it explains domination as a
  structural vulnerability and favors accountable institutions, participation,
  and checks on both public and private power.
- Bioregionalism treats ecological integrity and stewardship as political goods;
  it expects inherited borders or centralized management to miss ecological
  relationships and favors participatory governance and resilience at regional
  scales without requiring isolation.
- Political Islam treats Islamic principles as relevant to public authority or
  law, while its variants differ over jurisprudence, pluralism, democracy,
  constitutionalism, state-building, and coercion. The label does not settle
  one interpretation of Sharia or one relationship between religion and state.

These distinctions separate a community-centered critique, a civic freedom
tradition, a place-based ecological philosophy, and a diverse religious-political
family. They are editorial descriptions of the catalog profiles, not claims
that every movement using one of these names has the same complete program.

## Source basis

- “Communitarianism,” *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, Summer 2025
  Edition, on constitutive communities, social identity, tradition and context,
  and the normative value of community:
  https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2025/entries/communitarianism/
- “Republicanism,” *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, Fall 2025 Edition,
  on freedom as non-domination, civic self-government, rule of law, and the
  compatibility of republicanism with pluralist institutions:
  https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2025/entries/republicanism/
- Center for Bioregional Research and Regional Stewardship, SUNY College of
  Environmental Science and Forestry, on bioregions as ecological and cultural
  places, watershed boundaries, local knowledge, and participatory stewardship:
  https://www.esf.edu/cbbrs/about.php
- John L. Esposito and Emad El-Din Shahin, eds., *The Oxford Handbook of Islam
  and Politics*, Oxford University Press, on the geographic and ideological
  diversity of political Islam, including democracy, authoritarianism,
  movements in power, democratization, and jihadist political Islam:
  https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordhb/9780195395891.001.0001
- “On ‘Bioregionalism’ and ‘Watershed Consciousness’,” *The Professional
  Geographer*, on bioregionalism’s watershed and ecological orientation and the
  debate over its relationship to established ecology:
  https://doi.org/10.1111/j.0033-0124.1985.00001.x

The catalog’s decision to treat these as separate result profiles is an
editorial taxonomy choice informed by the sources. The sources do not imply
one universally settled classification for every communitarian, republican,
bioregional, or political-Islam movement.
