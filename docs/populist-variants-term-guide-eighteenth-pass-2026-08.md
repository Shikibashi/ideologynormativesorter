# Populist variants term-guide eighteenth pass

Date: 2026-08-11

## Scope

This pass reviewed four result names that still fell back to the generic
populism definition: Right-Wing Populism, Left-Wing Populism, Agrarian
Populism, and Cultural Populism. It adds direct guides, layer summaries, and
regression tests. It does not change centroids, axis weights, question
prompts, question selection, result eligibility, or scoring behavior.

## Direct term guides

- **Right-Wing Populism** now combines the people-versus-elite and popular-
  sovereignty frame with right-leaning hosts such as nationalism, nativism,
  traditionalism, or market conservatism. It is not a synonym for all
  conservatism or all anti-elite rhetoric.
- **Left-Wing Populism** now combines the same populist core with egalitarian,
  socialist, redistributive, or anti-oligarchic commitments. It remains
  distinct from social democracy, socialism, and generic anti-elite rhetoric.
- **Agrarian Populism** now identifies populism organized around rural
  producers or “people of the land,” with urban, financial, corporate, or
  state elites cast as exploitative. Agrarian populist movements can be
  progressive, conservative, socialist, or pro-market.
- **Cultural Populism** is explicitly treated as a catalog term for populist
  politics that defines the people and the elite through cultural identity,
  often opposing ordinary or traditional communities to distant,
  cosmopolitan, or institutionally powerful elites. It is not identical to
  right-wing populism or every defense of tradition.

Each guide is keyed to the stable catalog id and shown alone, so a specific
result is not displaced by the generic parent-family definition.

## Layer summaries

All four labels now have explicit normative, descriptive, and prescriptive
summaries:

- Right-Wing Populism emphasizes bounded or national membership and culturally
  conservative or nativist host frames.
- Left-Wing Populism emphasizes anti-oligarchic equality, redistribution, and
  popular mobilization without reducing the label to one socialist program.
- Agrarian Populism emphasizes rural producers and land-based livelihoods,
  while preserving the historical diversity of agrarian alliances and policy
  programs.
- Cultural Populism emphasizes cultural boundaries and perceived status loss;
  its economic and institutional positions remain open rather than being
  inferred from the cultural frame alone.

These summaries separate populism’s moralized people/elite antagonism from the
thicker ideology or social base that supplies a variant’s program. They are
editorial descriptions of the catalog profiles, not empirical claims about
every adherent or movement using any of the names.

## Source basis

- Cas Mudde, “The Populist Zeitgeist,” *Government and Opposition* 39.4
  (2004), on populism as a thin-centered ideology organized around the moral
  distinction between the people and the elite and popular sovereignty:
  https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1477-7053.2004.00135.x
- Cas Mudde, “Populism,” *The Oxford Handbook of Political Ideologies*, on a
  minimal cross-context concept of populism and its reliance on host ideologies
  for more complete policy content:
  https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordhb/9780199585977.001.0001
- Wirth et al., “Measuring Populist Attitudes on Three Dimensions,”
  *International Journal of Public Opinion Research* 30.2 (2018), on the
  people/elite opposition, popular sovereignty, and the way populism combines
  with thicker ideologies such as nativism or socialism:
  https://academic.oup.com/ijpor/article/30/2/316/2993904
- Chantal Mouffe, *For a Left Populism* (Verso, 2018), a primary left-populist
  intervention centered on constructing a democratic “people” against
  oligarchic power:
  https://www.versobooks.com/products/160-for-a-left-populism
- Arter, “Bigwig hatred and the emergence of the first Scandinavian
  agrarian-populist party,” *Scandinavian Political Studies* 46 (2023), on
  agrarian populism as an ideology rooted in rural-urban antagonism rather
  than one fixed property or class position:
  https://doi.org/10.1111/scps.12251
- Pye et al., “Three populisms and two dead ends: Variants of agrarian
  populism in Thailand,” *Journal of Agrarian Change* (2023), on the
  coexistence of progressive, reactionary, authoritarian, and pro-capitalist
  agrarian populist projects:
  https://doi.org/10.1111/joac.12504
- “Populism: A Socio-Cultural Approach,” *The Oxford Handbook of Populism*, on
  cultural identity, appeals, representation, and distinct left/right
  polarities in populist politics:
  https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/27977/chapter-abstract/211649560

The catalog’s decisions about which related traditions are required
philosophies, influences, or strategies are editorial taxonomy choices
informed by these sources. They are not claims that scholarship uses one
universally settled classification for every label.
