# Contested label research verification

This note verifies the labels that were marked **Needs narrowing** or **Use cautiously** in the academic audit. It records the research baseline used for the current wording in `src/data/labels.ts` and flags centroid follow-ups separately from text fixes.

| Label id | Current display label | Research baseline | Verification result | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `revolutionary-collectivist` | Revolutionary State Socialist | SEP/IEP-style socialism references distinguish democratic, revolutionary, state-socialist, council, syndicalist, and libertarian socialist traditions. | Current wording is acceptable because it narrows the label to revolutionary politics where centralized public ownership or state power is central, while explicitly separating council, syndicalist, and libertarian socialist traditions. | Later centroid review should confirm this is not too close to Marxist-Leninist, Maoist, or council-communist variants. |
| `neoliberalism` | Market-Governance Liberalism | Academic usage of neoliberalism is contested, but commonly covers market-oriented governance through competition, privatization/outsourcing, expert institutions, and state-making for markets. | Current wording is acceptable because it avoids using “neoliberalism” as a catchall insult and describes the narrower market-governance sense. | Consider a future subtype split for late-20th-century policy-regime neoliberalism vs. technocratic market liberalism. |
| `cyberocracy` | Cyberocratic Governance | Ronfeldt/RAND usage treats cyberocracy as a proposed information-age governance form shaped by information systems, electronic communications infrastructure, and organizational change. | Current wording is acceptable because it presents cyberocracy as experimental governance through information systems and decision-support rather than a settled ideology equal to liberalism or socialism. | Centroid should remain provisional until algorithmic governance, cybernetic planning, and expert-technocratic variants are separated. |
| `zionism` | Political Zionism | Scholarly and historical usage treats Zionism as Jewish nationalism/self-determination or statehood in the Land of Israel, with liberal, socialist, religious, revisionist, territorial, and post-Zionist debates. | Current wording is acceptable because it names political Jewish self-determination/statehood without implying one view on borders, religion, minority rights, or current governments. | Future centroids should split liberal, labor/socialist, religious, and revisionist Zionist variants if this area is used for precise results. |
| `hindutva` | Hindutva | Oxford scholarship identifies Hindutva as a contested term with multiple definitions; a common scholarly usage treats it as Hindu nationalism, while formulations range from moderate to extremist. | Current wording is acceptable because it distinguishes the political ideology from Hinduism as a religion and does not treat every adherent or formulation as identical. | Keep provisional and test civilizational identity, majority-community/state congruence, minority citizenship, and party strategy separately. |
| `strasserism` | Strasserite Fascism | Fascism scholarship treats fascism as palingenetic ultranationalism; specialist accounts of Strasserism place it inside Nazism/fascism despite anti-capitalist rhetoric. | Current wording is acceptable because it classifies Strasserism as a fascist far-right current and warns that anti-capitalist rhetoric does not make it socialism academically. | Consider whether the subtype should be grouped under a broader fascist family if families are later normalized. |
| `islamic-democracy` | Islamic Democratic Constitutionalism | Scholarship describes Islamic democracy as contested: Muslim-majority electoral democracy, Islamic constitutionalism, or democracy constrained by Islamic law may all be meant. | Current wording is acceptable because it narrows the result to electoral government, constitutional limits, and public accountability within an Islamic ethical/legal framework while distinguishing theocracy. | Future split could separate Islamic constitutional democracy, Muslim democratic parties, and theocratic politics. |
| `accelerationism` | Accelerationism | Academic and intellectual-history usage treats accelerationism as a family of left, right, unconditional, and technology-centered positions that intensify capitalism, technology, or modernity to force transformation. | Current wording is acceptable as a stopgap because it explicitly says the centroid is closest to technology-centered accelerationism and is provisional until variants are split. | Must split into left, right/unconditional, and technology-centered accelerationist centroids for higher precision. |
| `techno-anarchism` | Techno-Anarchist / Crypto-Anarchist | Anarchist theory centers skepticism of authority/power; crypto-anarchist and cypherpunk literature emphasizes encryption, anonymity, decentralization, and resistance to state/corporate information control. | Current wording is acceptable because it frames the label as an emerging overlap among cypherpunk, crypto-anarchism, and networked anarchism rather than as a canonical school. | Future split could separate crypto-anarchist, peer-to-peer commons, and broader techno-libertarian variants. |

## Source-backed ambiguity pass — 2026-08-12

The following labels already had bespoke usage or caution notes but lacked a label-specific public source record. The records now attached in `src/data/labelSources.ts` support the stated distinction or boundary; they do not validate a respondent centroid, establish that a label is a unified school, or substitute for expert and participant review.

| Label id | Source record | Supported distinction and retained limit |
|---|---|---|
| `technocratic-centralist` | Routledge, [Technocracy](https://www.rem.routledge.com/articles/technocracy) (`routledge-technocracy`) | Separates expert-centered political authority from the weaker claim that policy should use evidence and professional advice. The centralist subtype remains provisional. |
| `geolibertarian` | SEP, [Libertarianism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/libertarianism/) (`sep-libertarianism`) | Grounds the land-value and left-libertarian boundary without reducing geolibertarianism to generic market libertarianism or fixing one tax-and-property program. |
| `market-socialist` | Cambridge University Press, [Governing the Firm: Workers’ Control in Theory and Practice](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805218/18537/excerpt/9780521818537_excerpt.pdf) (`cambridge-market-socialism`) | Separates ownership from control and market allocation from social or public ownership. The label does not imply one firm-governance model. |
| `classical-liberalism` | SEP, [Liberalism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/liberalism/) (`sep-liberalism`) | Supports the rights, limited-government, and rule-of-law family boundary while avoiding the claim that all classical liberals share one economic or democratic program. |
| `ordoliberalism` | Cambridge University Press, [Adam Smith and Ordoliberalism: On the Political Form of Market Liberty](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/review-of-international-studies/article/abs/adam-smith-and-ordoliberalism-on-the-political-form-of-market-liberty/C09D6781C4E434370ED36FB4C66B3EAC) (`cambridge-ordoliberalism`) | Distinguishes a strong rule-setting state that constitutes competitive markets from laissez-faire. Welfare and democratic variants remain open. |
| `multiculturalism` | SEP, [Multiculturalism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/multiculturalism/) (`sep-multiculturalism`) | Separates cultural diversity as a fact from a normative program of recognition, accommodation, or group-differentiated rights. It does not imply separatism. |
| `radical-democracy` | SEP, [Global Democracy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2025/entries/global-democracy/) (`sep-radical-democracy`) | Supports participation, contestation, plural solidarity, and challenges to institutional closure as a boundary against a merely procedural electoral label. No single blueprint is asserted. |
| `one-nation-conservatism` | Wiley, [Such a Thing as Society](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1467-923X.13405) (`wiley-one-nation-conservatism`) | Grounds the historically British social-cohesion and welfare-state tradition while preserving disagreement about nationhood, immigration, sovereignty, and cosmopolitanism. |
| `democratic-confederalism` | University Press Library Open, [Democratic Confederalism](https://www.uplopen.com/books/m/10.1515/9783839472736) (`open-democratic-confederalism`) | Distinguishes the specific grassroots, feminist, ecological, confederal project from ordinary federalism or generic decentralization. |
| `liberal-feminism` | SEP, [Feminist Political Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/feminism-political/) and Routledge, [Liberal Feminism](https://www.rep.routledge.com/articles/thematic/feminist-political-philosophy/v-1/sections/liberal-feminism) (`sep-feminist-political-philosophy`, `routledge-liberal-feminism`) | Grounds equal legal and political rights as a liberal-feminist emphasis while locating it within a plural feminist field. It does not make legal equality the whole of feminism. |
| `left-wing-nationalism` | Oxford Academic, [A Political Theory of Decolonization](https://academic.oup.com/princeton-scholarship-online/book/14344/chapter-abstract/168273047) (`oxford-anti-colonial-nationalism`) | Supports an anti-colonial and self-determination boundary without treating left-wing nationalism as ethnic nationalism by definition. |
| `agrarian-populism` | Oxford Academic, [Agrarian Producerism after Populism](https://academic.oup.com/mississippi-scholarship-online/book/22613/chapter-abstract/182978326) (`oxford-agrarian-populism`) | Grounds rural producerism as a possible political combination rather than a fixed ideology; socialist, cooperative, racial-solidarist, and market variants remain distinct. |
| `green-capitalism` | Cambridge University Press, [Paying for Nature](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/oryx/article/paying-for-nature-what-every-conservationist-should-know-about-political-economy/069A96EEB80EA17E63D9A63DB6D4F236) (`cambridge-green-capitalism`) | Supports treating markets, payments, and commodification as contested tools in ecological political economy. It does not establish that market instruments are sufficient. |

## Source-backed subtype and context pass — 2026-08-12

These five labels received a second editorial pass because their existing definitions were accurate in outline but either too compressed or too easy to read as universal claims. The new copy adds bespoke boundaries; it does not promote any specialist or context entry into ordinary scoring.

| Label id | Source record | Supported distinction and retained limit |
|---|---|---|
| `anarcho-communist` | SEP, [Anarchism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/anarchism/) (`sep-anarchism`) | Supports treating anarcho-communism as one socialist-anarchist current among diverse anarchist traditions, with common ownership, free association, and disagreement over organization and strategy. It does not validate one transition model or centroid. |
| `minarchist` | SEP, [Libertarianism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/libertarianism/) (`sep-libertarianism`) | Grounds the minimal-state boundary and separates it from stateless anarcho-capitalism and broader libertarianism. The scope and justification of minimal state functions remain disputed. |
| `objectivism` | SEP, [Ayn Rand](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ayn-rand/) (`sep-ayn-rand`) | Supports attributing rational self-interest, individual rights, laissez-faire capitalism, and limited government to Rand’s Objectivism. These are author-specific doctrines, not generic libertarian or pro-market commitments. |
| `world-federalism` | SEP, [World Government](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/world-government/) (`sep-world-government`) | Distinguishes federal global authority from international cooperation, generic global governance, and a single centralized world state. Feasibility and institutional design remain open questions. |
| `bioregionalism` | Wiley, [A learning journey into contemporary bioregionalism](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/pan3.10548) (`wiley-contemporary-bioregionalism`) | Supports describing contemporary bioregionalism as plural, context-sensitive, and experimental rather than as one fixed map or theory of change. Its relationship to nation-states, markets, and capitalism is not determined by the label alone. |

## Source notes

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: liberalism (`https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/liberalism/`), socialism (`https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/socialism/`), anarchism (`https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/anarchism/`), nationalism (`https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nationalism/`), conservatism (`https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/conservatism/`), republicanism (`https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/republicanism/`), and democracy (`https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/democracy/`).
- Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy: socialism (`https://iep.utm.edu/socialis/`) and political philosophy methodology (`https://iep.utm.edu/polphil/`).
- Roger Griffin / Oxford-style fascism scholarship for fascism as palingenetic ultranationalism and fascist-current classification; e.g. Griffin's `The Nature of Fascism` summary (`https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Nature_of_Fascism.html?id=544bouZiztIC`) and a concise overview (`https://www.libraryofsocialscience.com/ideologies/resources/griffin-the-palingenetic-core/`).
- David Ronfeldt/RAND and related information-society scholarship for cyberocracy (`https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/papers/2008/P7745.pdf`; `https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/01972243.1992.9960123`).
- RAND's *The Emergence of Noopolitik* for information-age governance context and its distinction between emerging networked authority concepts (`https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/monograph_reports/MR1033/RAND_MR1033.pdf`).
- Scholarly/historical Zionism references treating Zionism as Jewish national self-determination/statehood while recognizing internal variants; e.g. Alan Patten's Princeton-hosted paper discussing Jewish self-determination (`https://apatten.scholar.princeton.edu/document/46`) and historical overview (`https://www.fpri.org/article/2015/01/origins-and-evolution-of-zionism/`).
- Cambridge's *Zionism* chapter for the tradition's changing relationship to nationalism, liberalism, socialism, religion, colonialism, and post-Zionist criticism (`https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-history-of-jewish-philosophy/zionism/27CF65008422154713C1885CB5EFDE3C`).
- Oxford's *Hindutva, Hindu Organizations, and the Hindu Diasporas* for contested definitions and the distinction between Hindu nationalism and Hinduism (`https://academic.oup.com/book/47098/chapter-abstract/416165265`).
- Islamic constitutionalism and Islamic democracy scholarship emphasizing contested meanings and institutional design (`https://scholarship.richmond.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1158&context=law-faculty-publications`; `https://www.e-ir.info/2015/01/07/what-is-islamic-democracy-the-three-cs-of-islamic-governance/`).
- Accelerationism scholarship and intellectual history distinguishing left/right/technology-centered variants, including the `#Accelerate` tradition and secondary scholarship (`https://www.urbanomic.com/book/accelerate/`; `https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0969725X.2019.1620464` where accessible).
- Cypherpunk and crypto-anarchism scholarship for the technology-mediated anarchist/current framing (`https://st-annes-mcr.org.uk/mcr-life/academic/staar/staar-7-2017/barret-bertelloni-2017-the-cypherpunk-vision-of-techno-politics/`; `https://www.researchgate.net/publication/380819410_Crypto-anarchy_a_paradigm_shift_for_society_and_the_legal_system`).
