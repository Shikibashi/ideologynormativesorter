# Label-source fidelity: seventeenth pass (2026-08)

This pass adds claim-matched sources for eleven provisional specialist labels that are frequently confused with broader families or neighboring traditions. These sources improve public exposition and boundary disclosure; they do not validate the app’s specialist centroids, assignment rosters, or empirical efficacy claims.

## Added specialist coverage

- **Neoconservatism** — Oxford scholarship on its heterogeneous domestic, constitutional, and foreign-policy arguments; it is not generic conservatism or interventionism.
- **Ecomodernism** — MIT scholarship on its technology-centered response to ecological crisis and its disagreement with degrowth and political ecology; technological decoupling is presented as a debated strategy, not a guarantee.
- **Socialist Feminism** — Cambridge historical research on the relationship and tensions between socialist and feminist movements, preserving disagreement over the relative significance of class and gender.
- **Christian Socialism** — Oxford history of Christian socialism’s relationship to labor, moral authority, and social transformation; it is not Christian democracy or theocracy by definition.
- **Guild Socialism** — Oxford research on self-managed producers, democratic consumers, and community institutions in G. D. H. Cole’s historical model.
- **Indigenism** — Oxford scholarship on international indigenous movements using human-rights institutions to resist assimilation and defend collective rights, autonomy, and self-determination.
- **Libertarian Municipalism** — peer-reviewed political-theory research on Bookchin’s variant of libertarian socialism; it is not ordinary municipal administration or generic decentralization.
- **Georgism** — Oxford scholarship connecting common claims to land with land-rent taxation and an otherwise competitive market order; land-value taxation is not treated as the whole of every Georgist position.
- **Paleoconservatism** — Cambridge research on its nationalist and identitarian current within American conservatism and its criticism of established elites.
- **Left-Wing Market Anarchism** — Routledge handbook coverage locating it between individualist/market anarchism and anti-capitalist or socialist currents; it is not interchangeable with anarcho-capitalism or market socialism.
- **Traditional Monarchism** — Oxford research treating monarchism as a family of arguments about hereditary rule and authority; traditional monarchism is not automatically absolutist, constitutional, clerical, or socially conservative.

## Scope discipline

Sources are limited to the claims their abstracts or descriptions support. The socialist-feminist, Christian-socialist, indigenist, paleoconservative, left-market-anarchist, and traditional-monarchist records are intentionally not used as evidence for prescriptive efficacy. All eleven labels remain provisional specialists and stay out of ordinary primary/modifier scoring.
