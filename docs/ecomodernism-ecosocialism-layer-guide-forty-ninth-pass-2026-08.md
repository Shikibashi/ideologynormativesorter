# Ecomodernism and Ecosocialism layer guide — forty-ninth pass (2026-08)

## Scope

This pass adds one descriptive-layer summary for `ecomodernist` and one for `ecosocialist`. It changes consumer-facing layer copy and its test only. No centroid, axis weight, question prompt, question selection, or scoring behavior changed.

## Layer decisions

Ecomodernism now has a descriptive summary that reports its characteristic expectation: technological modernization, urbanization, and intensified production can decouple human well-being from environmental impacts and spare land for nature. It does not present that expectation as established fact; the summary records the research distinction between relative and absolute decoupling and notes that the global, fast-enough absolute form required for ecological sustainability remains unestablished in the reviewed literature.

Ecosocialism now has a descriptive summary that reports its characteristic structural expectation: capitalist accumulation and profit-driven growth conflict with ecological limits, while collective ownership and democratic planning can orient production toward social needs and ecological equilibrium. The summary leaves room for disagreement over technology, institutional scale, and transition strategy rather than reducing ecosocialism to one administrative blueprint.

## Source basis

- The authors of _An Ecomodernist Manifesto_ describe ecomodernism's aim as decoupling human development from environmental impacts through technological, social, economic, and political modernization: https://www.ecomodernism.org/manifesto-english
- Wiedenhofer, Virág, Kalt, Plank, and Matej, “A categorisation and review of research literature,” _Environmental Science & Policy_ 112 (2020), review 179 studies and distinguish relative from absolute decoupling, finding no evidence of the global, fast-enough absolute decoupling required for ecological sustainability: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7330600/
- Michael Löwy, “Eco-Socialism and Democratic Planning,” _Socialist Register_ 43 (2007/2009), describes ecosocialism's focus on social needs, ecological equilibrium, collective ownership, democratic planning, and a critique of capitalist accumulation: https://socialistregister.com/index.php/srv/article/view/5869
