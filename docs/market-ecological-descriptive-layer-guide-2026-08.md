# Market and ecological descriptive-layer guide

Date: 2026-08-11

## Scope

This pass adds curated descriptive summaries for Agorism, Degrowth Green, and
Ordoliberalism. It does not change ideology scores, axes, question prompts,
result eligibility, or the existing normative and prescriptive layers.

## Layer decisions

- **Agorism** describes the expectation that counter-economics—voluntary
  exchange outside state licensing and taxation—can erode state legitimacy and
  build parallel institutions. The summary treats this as a specific Konkinian
  strategy rather than a generic synonym for market anarchism.
- **Degrowth Green** describes the expectation that indefinite growth and high
  material throughput in wealthy economies conflict with ecological limits and
  equitable well-being, while planned reductions, sufficiency, and democratic
  redistribution can improve ecological and social outcomes.
- **Ordoliberalism** describes the expectation that unregulated competition can
  produce monopoly and interest-group capture, while a strong rule-bound state
  can construct and maintain a competitive market order. It is distinct from
  both laissez-faire minimal-state liberalism and centralized economic
  planning.

These are descriptions of ideological expectations, not claims that the
expected outcomes are settled empirical findings.

## Source basis

- Samuel Edward Konkin III, *New Libertarian Manifesto*, a primary statement
  of counter-economics, voluntary exchange, and the strategy of undermining
  state authority:
  https://agorism.eu.org/docs/NewLibertarianManifesto.pdf
- “Agorism: Revolutionary Market Anarchism,” an archival movement source that
  identifies counter-economics as Agorism’s defining practice:
  https://agorism.eu.org/
- “Degrowth: From Theory to Practice,” *Journal of Cleaner Production*, on
  equitable downscaling of production and consumption, sufficiency, and
  ecological conditions:
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0959652612003162
- “15 years of degrowth research: A systematic review,” *Ecological
  Economics*, on reducing resource and energy throughput as a unifying theme
  while noting definitional and policy diversity:
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0921800923003646
- “Why a Handbook on Ordoliberalism?”, *Oxford Handbook of Ordoliberalism*,
  on competition as a state-constructed legal order rather than a spontaneous
  natural outcome:
  https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/44607/chapter/378218491
- “Competition Laws and Their Enforcement in the Project of European
  Integration,” *Oxford Handbook of Ordoliberalism*, on the strong state,
  anti-concentration policy, and competitive market order:
  https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/44607/chapter-abstract/378220425

The catalog keeps Agorism, Degrowth, and Ordoliberalism distinct because they
make different claims about markets, state power, ecological limits, and the
institutional conditions of coordination.
