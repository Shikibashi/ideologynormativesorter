# Technocratic, futurist, and green-technology term-guide sixteenth pass

Date: 2026-08-11

## Scope

This pass reviewed eight result names that still fell back to broad
technocratic, futurist, or environmental patterns: Technocratic Centralism,
Transhumanism, Cyberocracy, Accelerationism, Dataism, Singularitarianism,
Bright Green Environmentalism, and Green Capitalism. It adds direct guides,
layer summaries, and regression tests. It does not change centroids, axis
weights, question prompts, question selection, result eligibility, or scoring
behavior.

## Direct term guides

- **Technocratic Centralism** now identifies centralized expert administration
  and technical knowledge as sources of authority, distinguishing that
  stronger doctrine from merely using experts within democratic institutions.
- **Transhumanism** now identifies the family of views supporting technological
  expansion of human health, longevity, cognition, or other capacities. It
  notes disagreement over safety, access, equality, and governance, and keeps
  transhumanism distinct from posthumanism and generic technology enthusiasm.
- **Cyberocracy** now treats the label as a speculative theory of governance
  shaped by electronic information infrastructures and computational systems.
  It preserves the possibility of democratic, authoritarian, or hybrid forms
  rather than treating cyberocracy as synonymous with digital democracy.
- **Accelerationism** now identifies the contested family that treats
  intensification of capitalism, technology, or modernity as potentially
  transformative. Left, right, and technology-centered variants are not
  collapsed into one program.
- **Dataism** now identifies an emerging techno-philosophical term centered on
  data generation, processing, and flows, while explicitly rejecting the idea
  that it is a settled movement or that data are automatically neutral.
- **Singularitarianism** now identifies the futurist current centered on a
  possible technological singularity and on acceleration, preparation, or
  safety responses. It is not used as a synonym for all AI optimism or all
  transhumanism.
- **Bright Green Environmentalism** now identifies a technology- and
  design-optimist environmental current using innovation, infrastructure,
  urban design, and social or market transformation while retaining prosperity.
  It is kept distinct from both ecomodernism and green capitalism.
- **Green Capitalism** now identifies environmental protection pursued through
  capitalist markets, private investment, pricing, innovation, and corporate
  activity. It treats capitalism as a vehicle for ecological transition and
  records the continuing dispute over whether growth and profit incentives are
  sufficient.

Each guide is keyed to the stable catalog id and shown alone, so a specific
result is not displaced by a generic parent-family definition.

## Layer summaries

All eight labels now have explicit normative, descriptive, and prescriptive
summaries. These distinguish values, expectations, and practical strategies:

- The technocratic and cyberocratic entries separate the value of expertise or
  information capacity from claims about how computation will change
  bureaucracy and from proposals for centralized administration.
- The futurist entries distinguish enhancement, singularity expectations, and
  acceleration strategies rather than treating them as interchangeable forms
  of technological optimism.
- The green entries distinguish a technology-and-design orientation from the
  more specific market-capitalist mechanism of green capitalism.

These are editorial descriptions of the catalog profiles, not empirical claims
about every adherent or movement using any of the names.

## Source basis

- “Technocracy,” _Encyclopedia_ (MDPI, 2025), on governance by technical or
  scientific expertise and its tension with democratic judgment and intrinsic
  values:
  https://www.mdpi.com/2673-8392/5/4/194
- “Human Enhancement,” _Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy_, on enhancement
  enthusiasts and transhumanist arguments about transforming human nature,
  alongside bioconservative objections:
  https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/enhancement/
- Humanity+, “Transhumanist Declaration,” on overcoming aging, cognitive
  shortcomings, involuntary suffering, and the risks of technological change:
  https://www.transfigurism.org/about/official-documents/transhumanist-declaration
- David Ronfeldt, “Cyberocracy Is Coming,” _The Information Society_ 8.4
  (1992), on information and communications infrastructures altering
  bureaucracy and producing democratic, authoritarian, or hybrid outcomes:
  https://doi.org/10.1080/01972243.1992.9960123
- Alex Williams and Nick Srnicek, “#Accelerate: Manifesto for an
  Accelerationist Politics” (2013), a primary left-accelerationist text that
  helps establish why the broader label must not be treated as one uniform
  doctrine:
  https://391.org/manifestos/2013-accelerate-alex-williams-nick-srnicek/
- Jose van Dijck, “Datafication, dataism and dataveillance: Big Data between
  scientific paradigm and ideology,” _Surveillance & Society_ 12.2 (2014), on
  dataism as an ideological belief in datafication and the limits of trust in
  quantified systems:
  https://doi.org/10.24908/ss.v12i2.4776
- “A Trilemma for the Singularitarian,” _Philosophy & Technology_ (2023), on
  the singularity as a contested concept involving intelligence explosion,
  artificial general intelligence, superhumanity, and whole-brain emulation:
  https://doi.org/10.1007/s13347-023-00653-4
- Alex Steffen’s account of bright green environmentalism, reproduced with
  his definition of sustainable innovation, prosperity, design, urban
  revitalization, and entrepreneurial transformation:
  https://whattheythink.com/articles/77223-going-green-alex-steffen-worldchanging-com-speaks-seattle/
- Nick J. Fox, “Green capitalism, climate change and the technological fix: A
  more-than-human assessment,” _Sociology_ (2023), on green capitalism as a
  market-centered proposal and its incorporation of ecological conditions into
  capital accumulation:
  https://doi.org/10.1177/00380261221121232
- Jeffrey A. Ewing, “Hollow Ecology: Ecological Modernization Theory and the
  Death of Nature,” _Journal of World-Systems Research_ 23.1 (2017), on green
  capitalist and ecological-modernization approaches and their critics:
  https://doi.org/10.5195/JWSR.2017.611

The catalog’s decisions about which related traditions are required
philosophies, influences, or strategies are editorial taxonomy choices
informed by these sources. They are not claims that scholarship uses one
universally settled classification for every label.
