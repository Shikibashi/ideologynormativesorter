# Primary-label source fidelity pass — 2026-08

This pass closes the last four broad-primary labels that were relying only on
inherited family baselines: Democratic Socialism, Communitarianism,
Marxism–Leninism, and civic Republicanism. The new records support the label’s
definition and boundaries; they do not validate centroids or establish that a
respondent holds the tradition.

| Label | Direct source | Boundary preserved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Democratic Socialism | [American Democratic Socialism: History, Politics, Religion, and Theory](https://academic.oup.com/yale-scholarship-online/book/42901) | Historical and contemporary democratic-socialist traditions vary; this is not a claim that every social-democratic party seeks social ownership. |
| Communitarianism | [Communitarianism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/communitarianism/) | Community and socially formed identity are central themes, but communitarian positions vary over rights, markets, welfare, and state power. |
| Marxism–Leninism | [Marxism–Leninism as Discourse](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007123400005421) | Marxism–Leninism is treated as historically administered and redefined through party-state discourse, not as one unchanging national model. |
| Republicanism | [Republicanism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/republicanism/) | The public label means civic republican theory—especially non-domination and civic self-government—not a contemporary party label or one economic program. |

The source attachment tests now require these direct records and at least one
explicit layer-scoped record per scored label. Family baselines remain useful
orientation sources, but are restricted to definition and boundary claims so
they cannot be mistaken for subtype-specific evidence.
