# Revolutionary socialist descriptive-layer guide

Date: 2026-08-11

## Scope

This pass adds curated descriptive summaries for the catalog’s
`revolutionary-collectivist` umbrella and for Marxism-Leninism. It does not
change ideology scores, axes, question prompts, result eligibility, or the
existing normative and prescriptive layers.

## Layer decisions

- **Revolutionary Collectivist** describes a catalog-specific umbrella: it
  expects capitalist property relations to reproduce class domination and
  treats revolutionary seizure plus centralized collective ownership as a
  plausible transition mechanism. It is explicitly not presented as one
  historical school.
- **Marxism-Leninism** describes the expected role of a disciplined vanguard
  party and centralized state power in directing a transition toward planned
  social ownership and communism. The summary qualifies national and
  historical variation rather than treating every Marxist-Leninist state as
  institutionally identical.

These are descriptions of ideological expectations, not claims that the
proposed transition mechanisms have one universally established outcome.

## Source basis

- “Socialism,” *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, on socialism’s
  diversity, social or democratic control of productive assets, and the fact
  that socialism is not equivalent to one form of statism or planning:
  https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2025/entries/socialism/
- “Political Revolution,” *Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, on
  revolution as fundamental socio-political transformation and the role of
  revolutionary traditions in questions of power and transition:
  https://iep.utm.edu/pol-rev/
- Vladimir Lenin, *What Is To Be Done?*, a primary text on professional
  revolutionary organization and party leadership:
  https://www.marxists.info/archive/lenin/works/1901/witbd/index.htm
- Joseph Stalin, *The Foundations of Leninism*, a primary codification of
  Leninism’s party and revolutionary-state concepts:
  https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/stalin/works/1924/foundations-leninism/index.htm

The catalog’s use of `revolutionary-collectivist` is an editorial taxonomy
choice; it should not be read as a claim that the umbrella is a settled
academic category.
