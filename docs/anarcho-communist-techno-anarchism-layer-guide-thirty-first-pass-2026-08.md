# Anarcho-communist and techno-anarchist layer guide

Date: 2026-08-11

## Scope

This focused editorial pass adds the missing descriptive layer summaries for
`anarcho-communist` and `techno-anarchism`. Their direct term definitions and
normative or practical summaries were already specific. The new copy describes
each label’s characteristic expectations about social coordination and power;
it does not turn either label into a generic summary of anarchism. No
centroids, axis weights, question prompts, or scoring behavior changed.

## Layer decisions

- **Anarcho-Communism** now describes the expectation that decentralized
  communal production and sharing can meet needs and distribute socially
  generated wealth without centralized state control. It keeps feasibility,
  coordination, and the individual/community balance visible as contested
  questions rather than presenting a proposed future as an established fact.
- **Techno-Anarchism / Crypto-Anarchism** now describes the expectation that
  encryption, anonymity, distributed trust, and peer-to-peer networks can
  reduce centralized surveillance or gatekeeping. It also cautions that
  digital systems can create new concentrations of power and that this remains
  a loose current rather than a settled institutional model.

The labels are intentionally contrasted: the first is an economic and social
coordination tradition, while the second is a technology-mediated response to
centralized information control. The summaries do not imply that either
approach has one uniform historical program.

## Source basis

- “Anarchism,” *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, revised December 2025,
  on communist anarchism’s decentralized social organization, communal sharing,
  and the historical or empirical claims made about whether social life can be
  arranged without state intervention:
  https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/anarchism/
- Peter Kropotkin, *The Conquest of Bread* (1892), a primary anarcho-communist
  statement on communal production, social wealth, and distribution by need:
  https://theanarchistlibrary.org/library/petr-kropotkin-the-conquest-of-bread
- Enrico Beltramini, “The Cryptoanarchist Character of Bitcoin’s Digital
  Governance,” *Anarchist Studies* 29, no. 2 (2021), on autonomy, anonymity,
  distributed trust, and cryptoanarchist digital governance:
  https://doi.org/10.3898/AS.29.2.03
- Kevin Werbach, “The Blockchain and the New Architecture of Trust,” on the
  institutional tradeoffs and governance implications of distributed systems:
  https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.2980472
