# Religious nationalism and Hindutva layer guide

Date: 2026-08-11

## Scope

This focused editorial pass adds the missing descriptive layer summaries for
`religious-nationalism` and `hindutva`. Their direct term definitions and
normative or practical summaries were already specific, but their descriptive
layers fell back to broad family language. The new copy distinguishes a
comparative family of religious-nationalist projects from the India-specific
Hindutva label. No centroids, axis weights, question prompts, or scoring
behavior changed.

## Layer decisions

- **Religious Nationalism** now describes the expectation that shared religious
  identity can strengthen national solidarity and political legitimacy. It
  explicitly leaves open how movements connect faith to state authority, law,
  citizenship, and minority inclusion.
- **Hindutva** now describes the expectation that a culturally unified Hindu
  nation should organize political identity and public life in India. It also
  records the scholarly view that Hindutva is a modern and historically
  developing discourse whose relationship to religiosity, state power, and
  minority inclusion varies by organization and period.

These are descriptive accounts of characteristic arguments, not endorsements
and not claims that all religious nationalism or all Hindu political thought
has one institutional program.

## Source basis

- Jeff Haynes, “Religious Nationalism and Religious Influence,” *Oxford
  Research Encyclopedia of Politics*, on religious nationalism as a fusion of
  religious and national identities and goals, with varied implications for
  institutions, authority, policy, and political violence:
  https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/62239/chapter-abstract/550810397
- Mark Juergensmeyer, “Religious Nationalism and the Problem of Collective
  Representation,” *Annual Review of Sociology* 27 (2001), on religious
  nationalism’s distinctive account of solidarity and collective political
  representation:
  https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.soc.27.1.125
- Aparna Devare, “Secularizing Religion: Hindu Extremism as a Modernist
  Discourse,” *International Political Sociology* 3, no. 2 (2009), on
  Savarkar’s Hindutva as a modern political discourse centered on a homogenous
  Hindu nation:
  https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1749-5687.2009.00069.x
- Tanika Sarkar, *Hindu Nationalism in India* (Oxford University Press, 2022),
  on Hindutva’s historical development, organizations, cultural politics, and
  relationship to religious nationalism:
  https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780197645987.001.0001
