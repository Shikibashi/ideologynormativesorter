## `q0085` — labor-market coercion

The previous prompt grouped occupational licensing, immigration controls, and
zoning under one claim about labor-market coercion. Those are different legal
and institutional mechanisms with different purposes, affected populations,
and causal pathways. The new prompt is:

> Legal barriers that restrict workers’ ability to enter or leave employment can be forms of labor-market coercion.

The item context explicitly says that licensing, immigration status, housing
access, and employer power are distinct and does not imply that removing any
barrier automatically improves work. The [NBER study of occupational licensing
as a barrier to entry](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25262) supplies empirical
background on one mechanism, while the [ILO employment-relationship
recommendation](https://www.ilo.org/resource/other/r198-employment-relationship-recommendation-2006)
and labour-rights sources frame employment dependence and rights. These sources
inform interpretation; they do not answer the normative item.

## `q0407` — worker governance

The previous prompt combined productive-asset ownership and governance. The
new prompt is:

> Productive assets are most legitimate when the people who work with them have a direct governance claim over how they are used.

This isolates a worker-governance claim and leaves ownership form, capital
provision, management, compensation, investment, and firm performance for
other items. [Employee Governance and the Ownership of the
Firm](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/business-ethics-quarterly/article/abs/employee-governance-and-the-ownership-of-the-firm/F95BA42AF5F782A9BC16FC96FF6375F1)
is used because it distinguishes employee participation in decisions from
employee ownership. The Cambridge source on [structural domination and
freedom in the labor market](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/structural-domination-and-freedom-in-the-labor-market-from-voluntariness-to-independence/15DE09962D69039C60D94AE163381C72)
is a boundary source for coercive dependence, not evidence that one governance
arrangement is morally required.
