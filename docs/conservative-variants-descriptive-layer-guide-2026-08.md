# Conservative variants descriptive-layer guide

Date: 2026-08-11

## Scope

This pass adds curated descriptive summaries for Paleoconservatism, One-Nation
Conservatism, Fiscal Conservatism, Social Conservatism, National Conservatism,
Conservative Liberalism, and Liberal Conservatism. It does not change ideology
scores, axes, question prompts, result eligibility, or the existing normative
and prescriptive layers.

## Layer decisions

- **Paleoconservatism** describes the expectation that liberal
  internationalism, mass immigration, and multiculturalism weaken national
  cohesion and cultural continuity, while localism, economic nationalism, and
  non-intervention can protect the republic. The summary acknowledges internal
  variation and overlap with nativist politics.
- **One-Nation Conservatism** describes the expectation that social division
  and insecurity threaten national cohesion and that paternalist welfare or
  moderate intervention can bind classes together without abolishing
  competitive enterprise.
- **Fiscal Conservatism** describes a budget-policy expectation about debt,
  deficits, public spending, and sustainable public finances. It does not
  imply one social, economic, or foreign-policy program.
- **Social Conservatism** describes the expectation that rapid changes in
  family, gender, sexual, or religious norms weaken cohesion and moral
  formation, while inherited institutions and traditional authority stabilize
  community life. The content of tradition varies by context.
- **National Conservatism** describes the expectation that cosmopolitan or
  supranational institutions and rapid cultural change weaken national
  solidarity, while sovereignty and cultural continuity restore cohesion. It
  does not imply expansionist nationalism or one economic program.
- **Conservative Liberalism** describes a rule-based market-order synthesis:
  constitutional rules and prudent public authority are expected to preserve
  liberty better than unregulated market power or centralized direction.
- **Liberal Conservatism** describes a continuity-and-cautious-reform
  synthesis: constitutional rights and a market economy are expected to
  preserve inherited order without requiring reaction or wholesale redesign.

The final two summaries are deliberately distinguished. Conservative
Liberalism emphasizes disciplining markets and democracy through a liberal
constitutional order; Liberal Conservatism emphasizes preserving continuity
through liberal constitutionalism, cautious reform, and markets.

These are descriptions of ideological expectations, not endorsements or claims
that the expected outcomes are established facts.

## Source basis

- “Conservatism,” _Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy_, on conservatism’s
  skepticism toward wholesale redesign, its use of experience and tradition,
  and its historical variation:
  https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2022/entries/conservatism/
- “Political Philosophy: Methodology,” _Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy_,
  on conservative caution toward large-scale reform and respect for family,
  private property, religion, and intermediary associations:
  https://iep.utm.edu/polphil/
- Drolet and Williams, “America First: Paleoconservatism and the Ideological
  Struggle for the American Right,” _Journal of Political Ideologies_:
  https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13569317.2020.1699717
- Robert M. Page, “Towards a One Nation Conservative Welfare State?”,
  _Clear Blue Water_, on welfare, social reform, and the preservation of
  competitive enterprise:
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/clear-blue-water/towards-a-one-nation-conservative-welfare-state-the-conservatives-and-the-welfare-state-195064/27B06A5E7F337BF4201D04B31AE01839
- “Fiscal Conservatism,” _The Encyclopedia of Political Science_, on tax,
  spending, privatization, and fiscal-policy variants:
  https://sk.sagepub.com/ency/edvol/embed/the-encyclopedia-of-political-science/chpt/fiscal-conservatism
- “Protestants and American Conservatism: A Short History,” Oxford University
  Press, on family, church, customary hierarchy, and social-order questions:
  https://academic.oup.com/book/34883
- “What is National Conservatism?”, _Journal of Political Ideologies_, on
  national conservatism as a contemporary anti-liberal conservative paradigm:
  https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13569317.2025.2484524
- Kenneth Dyson, “Conservative Liberalism, Ordo-liberalism, and the State,”
  Oxford University Press, on rule-based market order and public authority:
  https://academic.oup.com/book/36150
- “Tocqueville, Burke, and the Origins of Liberal Conservatism,” _The Review
  of Politics_, on the historical rapprochement between liberal and
  conservative traditions:
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/review-of-politics/article/abs/tocqueville-burke-and-the-origins-of-liberal-conservatism/EA0357FB7B4F9113F665B865C8AB5830

The labels overlap historically, so the catalog keeps their summaries
qualified and avoids treating any one contemporary party or movement as the
definition of the whole family.
