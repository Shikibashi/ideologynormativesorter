# Ideology definition and boundary audit

Status date: 2026-08-19
Instrument: `clean-rebuild`
Scope: 16 primary labels, cross-cutting modifiers, and high-risk specialist boundaries

This is a source-backed conceptual audit, not a qualified-expert approval and
not evidence that the instrument classifies respondents accurately. The audit
keeps three questions separate:

1. What a tradition means in political theory and history.
2. Which normative, descriptive, and prescriptive constructs distinguish it
   from neighboring traditions.
3. Whether this instrument can measure those constructs reliably and fairly.

The first two can be researched from scholarship. The third still requires the
human review and respondent studies recorded in
`docs/research-readiness-2026-08.md`.

| Libertarian Socialism | Anti-authoritarian socialism, worker or community self-management, federation, and opposition to both capitalist concentration and centralized state socialism | Separate it from anarcho-capitalism, market anarchism, anarcho-communism, and generic decentralization by measuring property and coordination directly | Retain; experimental anarchist-family module |

| National Conservatism | National sovereignty and cultural continuity fused with conservative moral and social commitments | National identity does not by itself entail ethnic exclusion, religion, authoritarianism, or anti-democratic politics | Retain; experimental conservative-variant module |
| Radical Democracy | Expanded democratic contestation, constituent or popular power, plural conflict, and transformation beyond routine representative participation | Do not collapse it into generic democracy, anti-elite sentiment, direct democracy, or socialism; the theoretical family is internally contested | Retain provisionally; replace the current indirect source mapping |
| Republicanism | Civic self-government, rule of law, civic virtue, and liberty as non-domination or independence from arbitrary power | Keep it distinct from liberal non-interference, generic democracy, and state republican symbolism | Retain; current non-domination definition is well aligned |
| Social Democrat | Mixed economy and democratic institutions used to temper market outcomes through taxation, services, labor protection, and incremental reform | Do not treat any welfare-state preference as social democracy; distinguish reform strategy from social ownership | Retain; existing distinction from democratic socialism is sound |
| Social Liberalism | Markets and private property bounded by positive liberty, fair opportunity, and public provision or redistribution | Distinguish from market liberalism by substantive opportunity and from social democracy by the role of markets and the scale/form of socialization | Retain; keep positive-liberty items direct |

The broad-family architecture is therefore defensible. The scholarly record also
supports keeping the layers separate: normative democratic theory asks why and
when democracy is morally desirable, while descriptive democratic theory asks
how institutions function; the same separation is needed for ideology items.

## High-risk boundary findings

### Radical democracy source correction

The source mapping was corrected in this pass. `radical-democracy` now uses the
direct [Oxford Handbook chapter on radical democracy and constituent
power](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/62370/chapter-abstract/554752351)
rather than an indirect global-democracy entry. The source supports radical
democracy as a contested family involving constituent power, popular equality,
and democratic contestation beyond fixed representative institutions. The
label’s items and boundary note still require qualified review.

### Right-libertarian property boundary

The right-libertarian description now uses “rights-based private property
claims.” Use/occupancy, mutualist, Georgist, individualist-anarchist, and
left-market positions remain separate specialist constructs. This is a wording
fix, not psychometric evidence that the construct is cleanly separated in
respondent data.

### Liberalism, market liberalism, and neoliberalism

Liberalism is internally plural, including classical, social, positive-liberty,
and republican strands. Neoliberalism is also contested, but scholarship treats
it as a historically specific ideology, governance project, or regime rather
than a synonym for every market-friendly position. The current choice to keep
`neoliberalism` specialist/catalog-only is appropriate. Preserve the distinction
between a market-liberal primary family and a historical or governance-oriented
neoliberal specialist; do not classify by privatization or competition policy
alone.

### Nationalism, populism, and compound labels

Nationalism can combine with conservatism, socialism, Christianity, or other
full ideologies. Its core concerns nationhood, special obligations, and some
form of self-determination; civic, ethnic, religious, anti-colonial, and
regional forms must remain distinct. Populism is a thin-centred ideology whose
core is a moral opposition between a pure people and a corrupt elite, and it can
combine with nationalism, socialism, or ecologism. The current modifier roles for
`nationalism` and `populism` are therefore preferable to treating either as a
single primary ideology.

For `fascist-authoritarian`, `eco-authoritarianism`, `welfare-chauvinism`,
`religious-nationalism`, and `theocrat`, nearby axes are not defining measures.
The current catalog-only or opt-in disposition is correct until direct
construct-matched items are reviewed and tested.
