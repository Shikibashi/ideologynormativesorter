# Deep Ecology and Eco-Authoritarianism layer-guide twenty-eighth pass

Date: 2026-08-11

## Scope

This pass reviewed two ecological labels whose direct term guides were already
specific but whose layer summaries still relied on generic fallback copy. It
adds explicit normative, descriptive, and prescriptive summaries. It does not
change centroids, axis weights, question prompts, question selection, result
eligibility, or scoring behavior.

## Layer summaries

- **Deep Ecology** treats nonhuman life and ecological systems as possessing
  value independent of their usefulness to people. It expects anthropocentric
  industrial practices, pollution, and resource depletion to damage an
  interdependent biosphere, and favors restraint, ecological diversity,
  decentralization, and far-reaching social change around biocentric limits.
  It does not prescribe one population or economic policy.
- **Eco-Authoritarianism** treats ecological protection as an overriding
  political priority and accepts concentrated authority, restricted
  participation, or reduced individual discretion as possible means to secure
  it. It expects ecological crisis and the perceived slowness or fragmentation
  of democratic and market institutions to justify centralized expertise,
  command, and coercive environmental enforcement. Its prescriptive profile
  favors powerful centralized authority and command-and-control rules that can
  override ordinary democratic or individual constraints.

These summaries separate an ecocentric ethical and social critique from a
governance strategy that prioritizes ecological enforcement through
concentrated power. Environmental-authoritarian scholarship concerns varied
real-world regimes and does not imply that every case is identical to this
catalog’s ideological profile.

## Source basis

- “Environmental Ethics,” _Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy_, on Deep
  Ecology, biospheric egalitarianism, intrinsic value independent of human use,
  and the relational understanding of humans within nature:
  https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-environmental/
- Arne Næss, “The Shallow and the Deep, Long-Range Ecology Movement: A
  Summary,” _Inquiry_ 16 (1973), DOI 10.1080/00201747308601682, on the deep
  movement’s concern with diversity, complexity, autonomy, decentralization,
  symbiosis, egalitarianism, and classlessness:
  https://doi.org/10.1080/00201747308601682
- Sarah Eaton and Genia Kostka, “Environmental Authoritarianism: A Critical
  Review,” _The Oxford Handbook of Authoritarian Politics_ (2024), on the
  environmental-authoritarianism literature, including state capacity,
  co-optation, legitimization, repression, corruption, and resource politics:
  https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordhb/9780198871996.013.31
- Chen Xiang and Alex Lo, “Authoritarian environmentalism 2.0: An incremental
  transition of environmental governance in China,” _Environment and Planning
  C: Politics and Space_ 43 (2025), on the interaction of recentralization and
  decentralization in environmental governance rather than a simple binary:
  https://doi.org/10.1177/23996544241286325

The catalog’s decision to keep these as separate result profiles is an
editorial taxonomy choice informed by the sources. The sources support the
distinction between ecological ethics and authoritarian governance but do not
imply one universally settled classification for every environmental movement
or regime.
