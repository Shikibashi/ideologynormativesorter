# Editorial sixth pass — 2026-08-11

This pass continued the label-by-label and explainer review without changing respondent scores or pretending editorial judgment is psychometric evidence.

## Applied changes

- Replaced two compatible axis poles with actual contrasts: anti-domination now distinguishes continuing contestability from deference to established hierarchy, while the force axis distinguishes categorical pacifism from conditional moral permission.
- Added explicit layer-keyed summaries where reusable philosophy notes had conflated foundational values, empirical expectations, and practical strategy.
- Replaced missing-metadata language that falsely implied a tradition had no position with an honest statement about missing curated catalog copy.
- Corrected generated helpers for hyphenated occupational licensing, ordinary expertise, delegation of conscience, housing asset interests, and nonhuman moral standing.
- Renamed the label browser to describe the public subset it actually contains and distinguished primary, specialist, modifier, and related-tradition roles.
- Exposed the sources already stored for related traditions and disclosed that scored-label summaries do not yet have complete claim-level citations.

The conceptual baselines were the [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy on republican non-domination](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/republicanism/), its separate treatments of [war](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/war/) and [pacifism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pacifism/), the [U.S. Holocaust Memorial Museum on the Nazi state](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/foundations-of-the-nazi-state), and the tradition-level sources linked in the public catalog itself.

## Deliberately unresolved

- Label summaries remain editorial and contested. Related-tradition sources are now visible, but claim-level citations are not yet complete for every scored label.
- No question mapping, label centroid, or score was changed in this pass. Such changes require their own versioned instrument or scoring review.
- Software tests can establish data and rendering invariants only; they do not validate an ideology label as objectively correct.
