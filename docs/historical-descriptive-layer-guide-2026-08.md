# Historical descriptive-layer guide

Date: 2026-08-11

## Scope

This pass adds curated descriptive summaries for Kemalism and Christian
Reconstructionism. It does not change ideology scores, axes, question prompts,
result eligibility, or the existing normative and prescriptive layers.

## Layer decisions

- **Kemalism** describes the expectation that a secular, scientifically
  modernizing republic and unified Turkish national identity can overcome
  Ottoman and religious-political legacies. The summary qualifies variation
  over citizenship, pluralism, and the state’s role in modernization.
- **Christian Reconstructionism** describes the movement’s treatment of
  secular legal neutrality as a source of public moral disorder and its
  expectation that theonomic biblical norms can restore Christian public order.
  The summary qualifies variation over how literally and comprehensively
  biblical law should govern civil institutions.

These are descriptions of ideological expectations, not endorsements or
claims that the expected outcomes are established facts.

  https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-journal-of-middle-east-studies/article/abs/turkish-conservative-modernism-birth-of-a-nationalist-quest-for-cultural-renewal/72A93098038B9EC1D959659C08D0EAEB
- “Kemalism’s Desired Citizens,” in _Creating the Desired Citizen_, on the
  secular Turkish national project and its treatment of religious and Ottoman
  alternatives:
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/creating-the-desired-citizen/kemalisms-desired-citizens/3B5C41E0834DCECE9B9A11B7145A358E
- Julie J. Ingersoll, “Christian Reconstructionist Theology,” Oxford
  Research Encyclopedia / _The Oxford Handbook of Religion and American
  Politics_, on Calvinism, postmillennialism, presuppositionalism, theonomy,
  and the project of rebuilding Christian public order:
  https://academic.oup.com/book/10399/chapter-abstract/158211514

The catalog’s distinction between the descriptive, normative, and prescriptive
layers is editorial. The sources do not imply one settled interpretation of
Kemalism or Christian Reconstructionism.
