# Conservative-variant source pass — August 2026

This pass strengthens source fidelity for three public conservative variants that previously inherited only the broad conservatism baseline.

## National Conservatism

The catalog now cites [“What is National Conservatism?”](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13569317.2025.2484524), a recent _Journal of Political Ideologies_ article. The source is scoped to definition, descriptive characterization, and boundary. National Conservatism remains a distinct but developing contemporary ideological project; the source is not used to claim that all national-conservative movements share one economic, religious, or institutional program.

## Liberal Conservatism / Conservative Liberalism

The combined public entry now cites Kenneth Dyson’s Oxford study, [_Conservative Liberalism, Ordo-liberalism, and the State_](https://academic.oup.com/book/36150). It supports the historical cross-national synthesis of liberalism, conservatism, democracy, and a rule-based market order. It does not make Liberal Conservatism interchangeable with laissez-faire, generic conservatism, or ordoliberalism.

## Social Conservatism

The modifier now cites SAGE’s [_Social Conservatism_](https://sk.sagepub.com/ency/edvol/embed/the-encyclopedia-of-political-science/chpt/social-conservatism). The source supports the focus on preserving established moral, cultural, religious, and social norms while distinguishing that orientation from economic conservatism, Christian-right politics, and authoritarianism. Social Conservatism remains a cross-cutting modifier rather than a complete economic ideology.

The source scopes are intentionally narrower than the broad family baseline. They document interpretive boundaries; they do not validate the scoring centroid or establish respondent reliability.
