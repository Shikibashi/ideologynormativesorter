# Anarchist and anti-state term-guide fourteenth pass

Date: 2026-08-11

## Scope

This pass reviewed eight anarchist or anti-state result names that still fell
back to generic Anarchism or lacked a useful direct guide. It adds
label-specific definitions, layer summaries, and narrow metadata corrections.
It does not change centroids, axis weights, question prompts, question
selection, result eligibility, or scoring behavior.

## Direct term guides

- **Left-Wing Market Anarchism** now identifies an anti-capitalist market-anarchist umbrella that supports freed markets while opposing state-secured privilege and concentrated economic power. It is not treated as anarcho-capitalism or as a synonym for Mutualism.
- **Individualist Anarchism** now identifies a broad historical family organized around individual autonomy and voluntary association. Natural-rights, mutualist, and egoist currents remain distinct rather than being forced into Stirnerism, Egoism, or Post-Left Anarchism.
- **Anarcho-Primitivism** now identifies a specific anti-civilization critique of domestication, industrial technology, specialized labor, and mass organization. It is not used as a generic label for environmentalism, technology skepticism, Indigenous lifeways, or Kaczynski’s distinct anti-technology politics.
- **Voluntaryism** now preserves the distinction between Auberon Herbert’s voluntarily funded minimal “Voluntary State” and later anti-state Voluntaryist currents. The term therefore does not automatically mean anarchism or anarcho-capitalism.
- **Egoist Anarchism** now presents the anarchist reception of Max Stirner’s egoism and the contingent “union of egoists” without reducing it to ordinary selfishness, nihilism, or one complete policy program.
- **Anarcha-Feminism** now connects anarchist opposition to coercive hierarchy with feminist analysis of gender domination across intimate, economic, and political life.
- **Queer Anarchism** now identifies a diverse theoretical and activist current that links anarchist opposition to domination with resistance to enforced sexual and gender norms. It does not prescribe one universal account of identity, family, or gender abolition.
- **Techno-Anarchism / Crypto-Anarchism** now identifies loose currents using encryption, anonymity, peer-to-peer systems, and decentralized networks against surveillance, censorship, and centralized control. It is not treated as a synonym for blockchain advocacy or as a settled canonical school.

Each direct guide is keyed to the stable catalog id and shown alone, so a
specific result is not displaced by the generic parent-family definition.

## Metadata and layer corrections

- Left-Wing Market Anarchism no longer carries Mutualism as a required
  prescriptive philosophy; the related traditions remain named in its scope
  note and can still overlap.
- Individualist Anarchism no longer treats Stirnerism, Egoism, or Post-Left
  Anarchism as required subtypes, and its normative and prescriptive layers
  now carry the broader family label.
- Voluntaryism no longer uses “Contractual Anarchism” as an alias, preserving
  its historical state-supporting and anti-state variants as a contested
  lineage.
- Egoist Anarchism no longer carries Nihilism, Post-Left Anarchism, or
  Insurrectionary Anarchism as required taxonomy; its source-level Stirnerism
  and Egoism relationships remain explicit.
- Anarcha-Feminism now marks Anarcha-Feminism as prescriptive, while Queer
  Anarchism no longer presents Gender Abolition, Intersectional Anarchism, or
  Queer Liberation as mandatory subtheories. Queer Liberation remains an
  influence and prescriptive orientation.
- Techno-Anarchism no longer presents Cyber-Libertarianism, Cypherpunk, or
  Networked Anarchism as required subtheories. Cypherpunk remains an explicit
  related philosophy and prescriptive influence.
- The eight labels now have normative and/or prescriptive layer summaries
  where the catalog has a sufficiently identifiable practical orientation;
  these are editorial summaries, not empirical claims about adherents.

## Source basis

- “Anarchism,” _Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy_, on the diversity of
  anarchist forms and individualist anarchism’s emphasis on autonomy,
  self-determination, and voluntary cooperation:
  https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/anarchism/
- Gary Chartier, “Left-Wing Market Anarchism and Natural Law,” _Studies in
  Emergent Order_ 7 (2014): 314–324, on a contemporary left-wing market
  anarchist position:
  https://cosmosandtaxis.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/sieo_7_2014_chartier.pdf
- John Zerzan, “Future Primitive,” the primary essay associated with the
  anarcho-primitivist critique of civilization:
  https://theanarchistlibrary.org/library/john-zerzan-future-primitive
- Auberon Herbert, “The Principles of Voluntaryism and Free Life” (1897), on
  the voluntary state and opposition to compulsory taxation and political
  compulsion:
  https://www.panarchy.org/herbert/voluntaryism.html
- Max Stirner, _The Ego and Its Own_, on the unique individual and the
  contingent “Union of Egoists”:
  https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/stirner/ego-and-its-own.htm
- Donna M. Kowal, “Anarcha-Feminism,” in _The Palgrave Handbook of Anarchism_
  (2019), DOI 10.1007/978-3-319-75620-2_15, on the tradition’s emergence from
  anarchist women’s critique of sexual double standards and gendered labor:
  https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-75620-2_15
- C. B. Daring, J. Rogue, Deric Shannon, and Abbey Volcano, eds., _Queering
  Anarchism_ (2012), as described in Martha A. Ackelsberg’s author-archived
  preface on the book’s multiple critiques and alternatives:
  https://scholarworks.smith.edu/gov_facpubs/11/
- Timothy C. May, “The Crypto Anarchist Manifesto” (1988), and Eric Hughes,
  “A Cypherpunk’s Manifesto” (1993), as primary statements of cryptographic
  privacy, anonymous communication, and decentralized coordination:
  https://nakamotoinstitute.org/crypto-anarchist-manifesto/
  https://cypherpunks.venona.com/date/1993/03/msg00392.html

The catalog’s decisions about which related traditions are required
subtypes, aliases, or influences are editorial taxonomy choices informed by
these sources; they are not claims that the sources use one universally
settled classification.
