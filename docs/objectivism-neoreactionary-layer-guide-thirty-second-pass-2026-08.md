# Objectivism and neoreactionary layer guide

Date: 2026-08-11

## Scope

This focused editorial pass adds the missing descriptive layer summaries for
`objectivism` and `neoreactionary`. Both labels already had direct definitions
and normative or practical summaries, but their descriptive layer fell back to
generic related-tradition text. The new copy identifies the claims these
traditions make about knowledge and political institutions while marking them
as philosophical or author-specific rather than settled empirical findings. No
centroids, axis weights, question prompts, or scoring behavior changed.

## Layer decisions

- **Objectivism** now describes its epistemic realism and its expectation that
  rational individual production and voluntary exchange can coordinate social
  life. The summary explicitly says these are Objectivist philosophical claims,
  not a claim that social science has settled them.
- **Neoreactionary** now describes the current’s critique of electoral
  democracy as unstable, captured, or short-termist and its preference for
  concentrated executive or corporate governance with exit as an accountability
  mechanism. The summary preserves the label’s existing niche and
  author-specific caution.

These summaries describe what the traditions claim about how institutions or
knowledge work. They do not endorse the claims, generalize them to all
libertarian or authoritarian thought, or treat the labels as mainstream
academic schools.

## Source basis

- “Ayn Rand,” *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, on Objectivism’s
  objective/subjective/intrinsic distinctions and its claims about rational
  individual life and capitalism:
  https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ayn-rand/
- Gregory Salmieri, “Objectivist Epistemology in Outline,” Ayn Rand Institute,
  on Objectivist claims about objective reality and reason:
  https://courses.aynrand.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/OEIO-Transcript.pdf
- Joshua Tait, “Mencius Moldbug and Neoreaction,” in *Key Thinkers of the
  Radical Right: Behind the New Threat to Liberal Democracy* (Oxford
  University Press, 2019), on neoreaction’s anti-democratic, antiegalitarian,
  monarchic, and corporate-governance claims:
  https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780190877583.003.0012
- Mencius Moldbug, “A Formalist Manifesto” (2007), a primary statement of the
  corporate analogy and critique of democratic government:
  https://www.unqualified-reservations.org/2007/04/formalist-manifesto-originally-posted/
