# Stirnerism and Utopian Socialism Layer Guide — 2026-08

## Scope

This guide records the final descriptive-layer decisions for the two labels that remained intentionally uncurated after the earlier ideology-guide passes: Stirnerism and Utopian Socialism. The summaries remain descriptive rather than normative or prescriptive, and do not change scoring, axis definitions, prompts, or label eligibility.

## Stirnerism

The descriptive summary treats Stirnerism as a conceptual account of domination: fixed ideas can become independent authorities when abstractions such as Humanity, the State, or morality are treated as powers above the individual. It also recognizes contingent “unions of egoists” as coordination around participants’ own purposes. The summary explicitly says this is a philosophical account rather than a settled empirical model of social behavior, because Stirner’s text is a philosophical critique and does not establish a general social-science causal law.

Source basis: Max Stirner’s _The Ego and Its Own_ discusses the authority of fixed ideas and the treatment of abstractions as powers, while its account of association supports the existing union-of-egoists terminology: [primary text](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/stirner/ego-and-its-own.htm).

## Utopian Socialism

The descriptive summary identifies a qualified shared expectation across the tradition: deliberate reconstruction of society through model communities, cooperative experiments, and more scientific or rational understanding of social organization could demonstrate alternatives to competitive industrial order. It explicitly preserves the historical heterogeneity of Saint-Simonian, Fourierist, and Owenite projects, which differed in mechanisms and endpoints and should not be read as one institutional blueprint.

Source basis: Antoine Picon’s account connects nineteenth-century Utopian Socialism with attempts to develop a science of society in order to reconstruct it, while David Leopold’s historical discussion identifies Fourier, Owen, and Saint-Simon as the founding generation commonly grouped under the label: [Picon, _Utopian Socialism And Social Science_](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-history-of-science/utopian-socialism-and-social-science/BBC6DFE84C4258686107132EF64C4B80), [Leopold, _Marx, Engels and Some (Non-Foundational) Arguments Against Utopian Socialism_](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/edit/10.4324/9781315398068-4/marx-engels-non-foundational-arguments-utopian-socialism-david-leopold), and [Engels, _Socialism: Utopian and Scientific_](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1880/soc-utop/ch01.htm).

This guide supersedes the earlier intentional hold on both descriptive summaries. The hold was appropriate while no defensible shared mechanism had been documented; these qualified formulations now provide that mechanism without erasing conceptual limits or historical disagreement.
