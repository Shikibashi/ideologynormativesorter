## q0093 — worker status versus organizational form

The previous prompt treated employment, freelancing, cooperatives, unions, and partnerships as if they were parallel labor forms. Unions are collective organizations and partnerships are firm-level structures, while employee, independent-contractor, and cooperative-worker classifications concern different work relationships. The revised item asks whether basic protection should follow the facts of the work relationship, including where contractual labels disguise dependence.

Sources:

- [R198 Employment Relationship Recommendation, 2006](https://www.ilo.org/resource/other/r198-employment-relationship-recommendation-2006) — ILO guidance on distinguishing employed and self-employed workers, preventing disguised employment, and protecting workers across contractual arrangements.
- [Cooperatives and the Fundamental Principles and Rights at Work](https://www.ilo.org/publications/cooperatives-and-fundamental-principles-and-rights-work-cooperatives-and-0) — ILO discussion of cooperatives as employers and worker organizations.
- [ILO Helpdesk: Business and collective bargaining](https://www.ilo.org/resource/other/ilo-helpdesk-business-and-collective-bargaining) — distinction between worker organization, recognition, and collective bargaining.

## q0173 — one default-protection principle, distinct legal tests

The previous prompt bundled five rights without stating whether it was asserting one empirical relationship or one normative rule. The revised prompt makes the rule explicit: core liberties receive default protection, and restrictions need a specific public justification and legal test. The context note distinguishes ICCPR rights from encryption as a technical protection.

Sources:

- [International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights](https://2covenants.ohchr.org/About-ICCPR.html) — rights, remedies, limitation, and emergency provisions.
- [Guideline for Using Cryptographic Standards in the Federal Government: Cryptographic Mechanisms](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/175/b/r1/final) — technical context for encryption and cryptographic protections.
- [Liberalism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/liberalism/) — background on rights, liberty, and public justification.

## q0191 — plea-bargaining pressure without universal coercion

The previous wording said prosecutorial plea bargaining can pressure innocent or overcharged defendants to surrender trial rights without stating the jurisdiction, the mechanism, or the disagreement in the empirical literature. The revised item asks about a possible pressure mechanism while acknowledging that the size and direction of the effect vary and that factual innocence and counterfactual trial outcomes are hard to observe.

Sources:

- [The Innocence Effect](https://scholarship.law.duke.edu/dlj/vol62/iss2/3/) — empirical evidence that innocent defendants can be less likely to accept attractive offers than similarly situated guilty defendants, alongside a reported cost of innocence.
- [Plea Bargaining and the Miscarriage of Justice](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10940-019-09441-w) — real-case analysis of selection and miscarriage-of-justice concerns, with explicit jurisdictional and measurement limits.
