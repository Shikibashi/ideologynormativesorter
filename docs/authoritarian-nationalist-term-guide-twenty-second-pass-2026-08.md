# Authoritarian-nationalist term-guide twenty-second pass

Date: 2026-08-11

## Scope

This pass reviewed four labels that still fell back to broad conservative or
authoritarian definitions: National Traditionalist, Fascist / Palingenetic
Ultranationalist, Eco-Fascism, and Strasserite Fascism. It adds direct term
guides, layer summaries, and regression tests. It does not change centroids,
axis weights, question prompts, question selection, result eligibility, or
scoring behavior.

## Direct term guides

- **National Traditionalism** now identifies a conservative orientation that
  prioritizes national continuity, inherited institutions, social order, and
  cultural tradition. It remains distinct from ethnonationalism and fascist
  ultranationalism and does not determine one economic program.
- **Fascist / Palingenetic Ultranationalism** now uses the catalog’s narrow
  fascism definition: revolutionary ultranationalism promising national
  rebirth through authoritarian mass mobilization, hierarchy, anti-liberal
  politics, and coercive state power. It is not generic authoritarianism or
  ordinary conservatism.
- **Eco-Fascism** now identifies ecological politics joined to fascist or
  exclusionary ultranationalism, especially where ecological preservation is
  tied to demographic control, ethnic or national homogeneity, or coercive
  state power. Environmental concern or strong regulation alone is not
  eco-fascism.
- **Strasserite Fascism** now identifies the radical fascist current associated
  with the Strasser brothers and the Nazi movement, including anti-capitalist
  or anti-finance rhetoric within an ultranationalist authoritarian project.
  That rhetoric does not make the current socialism or generic market
  socialism.

Each guide is keyed to the stable catalog id and shown alone, so a specific
result is not displaced by a generic parent-family definition.

## Layer summaries

All four labels now have explicit normative, descriptive, and prescriptive
summaries:

- National Traditionalist emphasizes national continuity, inherited authority,
  cultural tradition, and cautious reform without inferring fascism or racial
  exclusion.
- Fascist / Palingenetic Ultranationalist emphasizes organic national unity,
  rebirth, hierarchy, discipline, mass mobilization, and coercive anti-liberal
  power.
- Eco-Fascism emphasizes a bounded national or ethnic ecology enforced through
  exclusionary or authoritarian ecological measures rather than ordinary
  environmental regulation.
- Strasserite Fascism emphasizes fascist national rebirth and mass
  mobilization while treating anti-capitalist or corporatist measures as
  subordinate to ultranationalism.

These distinctions separate a conservative national-traditionalist tendency,
an ideal-typical fascist form, an ecological far-right subtype, and a
historically specific fascist current. They are editorial descriptions of
the catalog profiles, not claims that every movement using one of these names
has the same complete program.

## Source basis

- Roger Griffin, *Fascism*, Oxford University Press, on comparative fascism
  and the revolutionary ultranationalist form of fascist politics:
  https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780192892492.001.0001
- Roger Griffin’s Oxford Brookes profile, on palingenetic myth as a theory of
  fascism and its use in comparative fascist studies:
  https://www.brookes.ac.uk/profiles/staff/roger-griffin
- Daniele Conversi, “Eco-fascism: an oxymoron? Far-right nationalism, history,
  and the climate emergency,” *Frontiers in Human Dynamics* 6 (2024), on the
  contested term and the relationship between far-right nationalism and
  environmental politics:
  https://doi.org/10.3389/fhumd.2024.1373872
- Sam Moore and Alex Roberts, “Ecofascism, far-right ecologism, and
  neo-Malthusianism,” *The New Political Economy of the Far Right*, on
  ecofascism as a cluster of far-right ecological discourses rather than a
  synonym for environmentalism:
  https://doi.org/10.7765/9781526173898.00011
- Peter King, “The idea of conservatism,” on traditionalism, skepticism,
  organicism, and the conservative concern with established institutions:
  https://doi.org/10.1332/policypress/9781847428547.003.0002
- J. Simpson, “It Was Always Blood and Soil: Ecofascism and the Racial
  Capitalocene,” *Antipode* 57, on the racialized ecological politics captured
  by some uses of ecofascism:
  https://doi.org/10.1111/anti.13134

The catalog’s decision to treat these as separate result profiles is an
editorial taxonomy choice informed by the sources. The sources do not imply
one universally settled classification for every conservative, fascist,
ecofascist, or Strasserist movement.
