# Specialist layer and Islamic constitutionalism pass — 2026-08

This pass closes two measurement-contract gaps found during the specialist audit.

## Layer alignment

Specialist questions carry compatibility weights for the global axis model, even though specialist classification uses local construct weights. Several older items mixed normative, descriptive, and prescriptive axis IDs. That made the specialist corpus violate the same layer contract enforced for the core bank and could silently attribute a descriptive answer to a normative or policy axis.

The compatibility weights are now layer-aligned. The local specialist constructs are unchanged except where the religious-national module was expanded. A regression test now checks every specialist question against the axis registry and rejects duplicate specialist question IDs.

## Islamic democratic constitutionalism and neighboring projects

The religious-national module previously measured popular constitutional accountability, religious authority, civilizational nationalism, and minority citizenship. Research on Islamic constitutionalism treats constitutional design as a site of negotiation among popular sovereignty, constitutional limits, interpretation of Islamic principles, and the authority of courts or other guardians. Comparative work also distinguishes whether Islamic parties accept plural competition and peaceful alternation rather than treating “Islamic politics” as one regime type.

The module now adds two separately scored experimental constructs:

- `constitutional-review`: whether constitutional review can constrain legislation that violates protected rights, including when an elected majority supports it;
- `party-competition`: whether parties and candidates may compete and replace governments peacefully under equal-citizenship safeguards.

These remain opt-in experimental constructs. Their candidate profiles are hypotheses for pilot measurement, not validated ideological identities, and they do not promote Islamic Democratic Constitutionalism, Political Islam, Hindutva, Zionism, Religious Nationalism, Christian Reconstructionism, or theocracy-related labels into ordinary scoring.

Research anchors:

- [Islamic Constitutionalism (Annual Review of Law and Social Science)](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.lawsocsci.3.081806.112753) — distinguishes phases of constitutional incorporation and limited government.
- [Islam and Constitutional Law (Cambridge University Press)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/17C6F64060962A75D71F5475F6918689/9781009286046c19_360-384.pdf/islam_and_constitutional_law.pdf) — describes Islamic review and the distinct questions of interpretation, judicial authority, and public welfare.
- [Constitutionalism, judiciary, and democracy in Islamic societies](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/constitutionalism-judiciary-and-democracy-in-islamic-societies/) — frames judicial review and rights protection as a tension between constitutionalism and majority rule.
- [From Islamists to Muslim Democrats: The Case of Tunisia’s Ennahda](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/abs/from-islamists-to-muslim-democrats-the-case-of-tunisias-ennahda/C0D3D82CA222E3C28B108B28ED5A4DD4) — provides a case where party positions on democracy, constitutional religion, and freedom of conscience changed over time.

Literature and module items still require cognitive interviews, sample-based reliability, test–retest evidence, criterion comparisons, and fairness review before any specialist promotion.
