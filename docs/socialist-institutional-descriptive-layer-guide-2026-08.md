# Socialist institutional descriptive-layer guide

Date: 2026-08-11

## Scope

This pass adds curated descriptive summaries for Council Communism,
Syndicalism, Libertarian Socialism, and Participism. It does not change
ideology scores, axes, question prompts, result eligibility, or the existing
normative and prescriptive layers.

## Layer decisions

- **Council Communism** describes the expectation that workers’ councils and
  federated direct control can replace both capitalist hierarchy and
  party-state substitution as the basis of production and political power.
- **Syndicalism** describes the expectation that autonomous labor
  organizations and direct action can build class power more effectively than
  electoral parties or state administration, with federated unions providing
  a basis for post-capitalist coordination.
- **Libertarian Socialism** describes a broad expectation that both capitalist
  concentration and centralized party-state control reproduce domination, and
  that worker self-management, voluntary federation, and social ownership can
  coordinate production without vanguard seizure.
- **Participism** describes Albert and Hahnel’s specific participatory
  economics model: worker and consumer councils, balanced job complexes, and
  negotiated planning. It is not treated as a synonym for every participatory
  or libertarian-socialist tradition.

These are descriptions of ideological and institutional expectations, not
claims that any proposed model has one settled empirical outcome.

## Source basis

- “Socialism,” *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, on the diversity of
  socialist models, social control of productive assets, and disagreements
  about markets, planning, and democratic control:
  https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/socialism/index.html
- “Socialism,” *Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, including its treatment
  of participatory economics as a developed participatory-planning model:
  https://iep.utm.edu/socialis/
- “Direct Action: The Invention of a Transnational Concept,” *International
  Review of Social History*, on revolutionary syndicalism’s combination of
  trade unionism and direct action:
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-review-of-social-history/article/direct-action-the-invention-of-a-transnational-concept/12505D4E6288B6BC01859740280AA6D4
- “Building Workers’ Power,” in *Building Power to Change the World*, on
  German council movements and worker-led organizations:
  https://academic.oup.com/book/40367/chapter/347057694
- Sean Scalmer, “The Inconceivability of Real ‘Workers’ Control’ under
  Capitalism,” *International Labor and Working-Class History*, on Pannekoek,
  council communism, and workers’ councils:
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/4FE0AB81C4EA9208333A4D81AE593887/S0147547922000308a.pdf/the-inconceivability-of-real-workers-control-under-capitalism.pdf
- Michael Albert and Robin Hahnel, *The Political Economy of Participatory
  Economics*, on democratic planning through producers’ and consumers’
  councils:
  https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/9780691216003/html?lang=en

The catalog keeps these labels separate because council authority, union
organization, broad anti-authoritarian socialism, and the specific Parecon
model are not interchangeable institutional claims.
