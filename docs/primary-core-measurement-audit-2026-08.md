# Primary-core measurement audit — 2026-08

This is a current executable-code audit for taxonomy version `2026-08-taxonomy-v13` and primary measurement version `2026-08-primary-core-v1`. It supersedes neither the historical breadth records nor the specialist-validation protocol. Its narrower purpose is to document what the ordinary primary score is permitted to compare after the source-backed core-scope migration.

## Current state

The registry contains 16 ordinary primaries, 24 modifiers, 78 specialists, 19 context entries, and 8 retired compatibility entries. Thirty-nine specialists have a construct-matched opt-in module; the remaining 39 are browsable provisional entries. A specialist centroid, historical importance, or synthetic fixture does not put that label into ordinary results.

Every ordinary primary now declares:

- comparison axes that are narrower than its legacy full centroid;
- required axes that must be measured before it can appear;
- a source record disclosed on the result card; and
- a limitation describing what the ordinary bank cannot establish.

The source record supports the label's definition or boundary. It does not validate a numeric centroid, establish that a respondent identifies with the tradition, or substitute for respondent validation.

Oxford's overview treats liberalism as internally plural: its basic institutions can include constitutionalism, markets, public goods, and a social minimum, while classical, libertarian, and high-liberal accounts place those elements differently. [Oxford Research Encyclopedia, *Liberalism*](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/62239/chapter-abstract/550776269) SEP likewise describes classical liberalism as a spectrum and distinguishes a stronger libertarian skepticism of political authority and coercion from broader liberal families. [SEP, *Liberalism*](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/liberalism/) [SEP, *Libertarianism*](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/libertarianism/)

These sources support retaining a broad market-liberal comparison and considering authority in its boundary. They do not determine a unique numerical separation between market liberalism, classical liberalism, and right-libertarianism. That is an empirical validation question.

## High-risk false-positive policy

The ordinary result path can show Marxism-Leninism only because it is a primary with both a source-backed core scope and a constitutive gate: socialist property commitments, centralized authority, revolutionary change, and state transition must all be measured. Egalitarianism plus state action is not enough.

The following named traditions are excluded from both ordinary primary and ordinary modifier output: Fascism, National Socialism, Strasserism, Eco-Fascism, Ethnonationalism, Maoism, National Bolshevism, Christian Reconstructionism, Fundamentalist Theocracy, Political Islam, Anarcho-Capitalism, Anarcho-Communism, Welfare Chauvinism, and Georgist Libertarianism. They remain specialist, focused-follow-up, or catalog-only entries with their own construct gates where an opt-in module exists.

This keeps generic combinations such as nationalism plus social conservatism, environmental concern plus nationalism, religiosity plus traditionalism, or markets plus distrust of government from becoming a narrowly historic result.

## Deliberate limitations and deferred item work

No new global axes were added in this pass. The audit found real construct gaps, but adding an axis before cognitive review and question development would create false precision.

| Primary or family | Missing construct | Safe current behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Republicanism | civic self-government and institutional independence from arbitrary power | Compare only the currently measured non-domination boundary; do not select a republican institutional model. |
| Radical Democracy | popular sovereignty and participatory self-government | Keep the ordinary result constrained; do not infer it from electoral confidence. |
| Prudential Conservatism | institutional prudence, inherited practice, and non-rationalist reform judgment | Use the narrow gradual-change comparison and abstain when it is unmeasured. |
| Marxian Socialism | class structure and historical-materialist explanation | Do not treat the ordinary score as a complete Marxian theory measure. |
| Green Politics | growth, technology, political economy, governance, and strategy | Keep the ordinary signal to moral standing and use the opt-in green morphology module for multi-affinity distinctions. |
| Market Liberalism / Classical Liberalism / Right-Libertarianism | finer institutional limits, public-goods theory, property acquisition, and state-function differences | Show a broad neighborhood and evidence/uncertainty, not a validated identity diagnosis. |
| Democratic Socialism / Marxian Socialism | democratic workplace control, class analysis, and historical-materialist explanation | Their current ordinary cores substantially overlap. Treat a close ordering as an uncertain neighborhood; add and validate dedicated constructs before claiming finer separation. |
| Social / Communal Anarchism | the precise property, organizational, and strategic boundaries among communal, mutualist, syndicalist, and market anarchist currents | Keep it specialist-only, with a family-level opt-in anarchist module. It is not a parent of Mutualism or Market Anarchism. |

The first four limitations follow from the familiar distinctions in the scholarly literature: republican freedom is not reducible to non-interference, conservatism need not mean reaction, and socialism spans distinct ownership, organizational, and transition theories. [SEP, *Republicanism*](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/republicanism/) [SEP, *Conservatism*](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/conservatism/) [SEP, *Socialism*](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/socialism/)

## Required empirical follow-up

Before any primary centroid, threshold, or specialist role is described as accurate rather than provisional, the project still needs cognitive interviews, response-process review, test-retest results, calibration against independently supplied criteria, and fairness/DIF analysis. Synthetic profiles and automated tests only verify the implementation contract: they do not establish how respondents understand the items or whether a label predicts a stable self-description.
