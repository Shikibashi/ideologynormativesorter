# Liberal-family term-guide twenty-third pass

Date: 2026-08-11

## Scope

This pass reviewed five liberal-family labels that still fell back to broad
liberal, cosmopolitan, or technocratic definitions: Progressivism, Liberal
Feminism, Georgism, Internationalism, and Radical Centrism. It adds direct
term guides, layer summaries, and regression tests. It does not change
centroids, axis weights, question prompts, question selection, result
eligibility, or scoring behavior.

## Direct term guides

- **Progressivism** now identifies a broad and historically changing reform
  tradition seeking deliberate social improvement through inquiry, institutional
  experimentation, public policy, and sometimes participatory movements. It is
  not synonymous with technocracy or one contemporary policy platform.
- **Liberal Feminism** now identifies a rights- and autonomy-centered approach
  to gender equality pursued through legal reform and equal opportunity within
  liberal-democratic institutions. It remains distinct from feminist traditions
  centered on capitalist property, social reproduction, or patriarchy as
  structures requiring broader transformation.
- **Georgism** now distinguishes privately created improvements from the social
  value of land and natural opportunities and generally favors public capture
  of land or resource rent. The historic single-tax proposal is one formulation
  rather than the whole tradition.
- **Internationalism** now identifies cooperation, obligations, and rights
  across national boundaries through institutions, treaties, solidarity, or
  universal rights. It is broader than cosmopolitanism and does not require
  one world government.
- **Radical Centrism** now identifies a contested pragmatic style that seeks
  cross-ideological synthesis and institutional reform. “Radical” describes
  cross-cutting problem-solving here, not necessarily rupture or one policy
  program.

Each guide is keyed to the stable catalog id and shown alone, so a specific
result is not displaced by a generic parent-family definition.

## Layer summaries

All five labels now have explicit normative, descriptive, and prescriptive
summaries:

- Progressivism emphasizes deliberate improvement, empirical inquiry, reform,
  public action, and the variation between expert-led and participatory
  progressive movements.
- Liberal Feminism emphasizes equal rights, autonomy, legal status, opportunity,
  anti-discrimination protections, and institutional reform without prescribing
  one economic theory of patriarchy.
- Georgism treats labor and improvements differently from socially generated
  land rent and favors land-value or related rent capture while leaving the
  single-tax formulation and service design open to variation.
- Internationalism emphasizes cross-border obligations and cooperation while
  preserving the possibility of national self-government and rejecting the
  assumption that internationalism requires a world state.
- Radical Centrism emphasizes practical problem-solving, pluralist compromise,
  evidence, experimentation, and cross-partisan reform without pretending that
  the label supplies one complete economic program.

These distinctions separate a broad reform tradition, a liberal feminist strand,
a land-rent theory, a cross-border political orientation, and a contested
centrist style. They are editorial descriptions of the catalog profiles, not
claims that every movement using one of these names has the same complete
program.

## Source basis

- Ronald Hayduk, “Progressives,” _International Encyclopedia of the Social
  Sciences_, on progressivism’s diverse reform coalitions, institutional
  reform, public-interest claims, and changing historical forms:
  https://www.encyclopedia.com/social-sciences/applied-and-social-sciences-magazines/progressives
- David L. Cockrell and Allyson C. Criner, “Progressivism,” _Encyclopedia of
  North Carolina_, on the term’s changing forms and its association with
  educational, economic, and social development:
  https://www.ncpedia.org/progressivism
- Ann Cudd, “Liberal Feminism,” _Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy_, on
  personal and political autonomy, equality, anti-discrimination, and the role
  of state institutions in egalitarian-liberal feminism:
  https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/feminism-liberal/
- Linda C. McClain and Brittany K. Hacker, “Liberal Feminist Jurisprudence:
  Foundational, Enduring, Adaptive,” _The Oxford Handbook of Feminism and Law
  in the United States_, on autonomy, dignity, equality, and legal reform:
  https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordhb/9780197519998.013.2
- Henry George, _Progress and Poverty_, Chapters 11 and 33, on the distinction
  between land rent and human improvements and the case for taxing land value:
  https://www.henrygeorge.org/pchp11.htm
  https://www.henrygeorge.org/pchp33.htm
- H. James Brown, ed., _Land Use and Taxation: Applying the Insights of Henry
  George_, Lincoln Institute of Land Policy, on the continuing policy debate
  over private land rights, public interests, land value, and value capture:
  https://www.lincolninst.edu/publications/books/land-use-taxation/
- Anthony F. Lang Jr., “Internationalism,” _International Encyclopedia of the
  Social Sciences_, on internationalism’s overlapping meanings, international
  law and institutions, its distinction from cosmopolitanism, and its
  difference from world government:
  https://www.encyclopedia.com/social-sciences-and-law/political-science-and-government/military-affairs-nonnaval/internationalism
- Talbot Imlay, _The Practice of Socialist Internationalism: European Socialists
  and International Politics, 1914–1960_, Oxford University Press, on
  internationalism operating through both national and transnational spaces:
  https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780199641048.001.0001
- Karl Pike, “What Is Centrism?” _Political Studies_, on centrism’s changeable
  and contested character, its strategic relationship to left and right, and
  its pragmatic emphasis on reforms blocked by ideological intransigence:
  https://doi.org/10.1177/00323217251410692

The catalog’s decision to treat these as separate result profiles is an
editorial taxonomy choice informed by the sources. The sources do not imply
one universally settled classification for every progressive, liberal-feminist,
Georgist, internationalist, or radical-centrist movement.
