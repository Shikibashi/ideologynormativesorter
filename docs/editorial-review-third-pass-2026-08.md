# Ideology and question review — third pass (2026-08)

## Scope and decision rule

This pass continued the label-by-label and item-by-item review after the second-pass audit. It corrects display taxonomy when a label repeated itself, treated an alias as a child school, or grouped a neighboring idea as a subtype. It also applies wording-only question edits when the original construct and scoring direction remain clear.

No centroid or question axis weight changed. Replacement proposals are review notes, not active questions. This remains editorial content review rather than evidence of reliability, dimensionality, criterion validity, or respondent comprehension.

## Ideology metadata corrections

- `cyberocracy` now stays focused on governance shaped by information systems, cybernetics, technocracy, and algorithmic administration. Generic Progressivism is not entailed by that concept. Netocracy is a separate social-order claim, while Syntheism describes itself as a process religion/process philosophy rather than a cyberocratic subtype.
- `hindutva` no longer lists Hindu Nationalism, Saffronization, and Cultural Nationalism as child schools. Hindu nationalism is already part of the label’s defining scope, “saffronization” is used in scholarship for a political and institutional process, and cultural nationalism is broader than this one ideology.
- `zionism` no longer repeats Political Zionism as a subtype of Political Zionism. Labour, Religious, and Revisionist Zionism remain as historically distinct currents.
- `islamic-democracy` now belongs to the existing `religious-political` family. Its layer metadata uses Islamic Democracy and Religious Constitutionalism rather than implying that every democratic Islamic constitutional position is generic Conservatism or Islamism. Post-Islamism and Muslim Democracy are no longer presented as child schools, and the label now receives its own constitutional-democratic explainer instead of the generic Political Islam family explainer.
- `corporatism` is displayed as State Corporatism, matching its authoritarian family, description, and usage note. Its explainer now distinguishes that state-directed form from democratic societal or neo-corporatist bargaining.
- `world-federalism` no longer treats Cosmopolitan Democracy or Global Governance as children. It is explicitly provisional until a global-democracy depth module exists and is no longer routed through nationalist-module questions.
- `liquid-democracy`, `bleeding-heart-libertarianism`, `dataism`, and `singularitarianism` no longer present alternate terminology, broader philosophical families, policy techniques, research fields, or critical theories as child ideologies. Bare “Delegative Democracy” metadata was also removed from Liquid Democracy because that term has a separate executive-rule usage in comparative politics.
- `regionalism` now uses a regionalist rather than nationalist display family and no longer treats devolution, autonomism, or the separately cataloged ecological philosophy Bioregionalism as child ideologies.
- `civic-nationalist`, `anarcho-primitivism`, `paleoconservatism`, `traditional-monarchist`, and `theocrat` no longer present neighboring traditions, parent families, legitimation doctrines, governance forms, or religion-specific variants as child schools or aliases.
- Self-repetition or alias/subtype overlap was removed from `left-wing-market-anarchism`, `guild-socialism`, `national-bolshevism`, `strasserism`, `accelerationism`, `national-socialism`, `utopian-socialism`, `voluntaryism`, `kemalism`, `national-conservatism`, `liberal-conservatism`, and `radical-centrism`. Radical Center remains available as an alias rather than as a subtype of Radical Centrism.
- Filler values such as “Internal Strands,” “Internal Traditions,” “Internal Schools,” and “Internal Branches” were removed from `ecosocialist`, `conservative-liberalism`, `trotskyism`, and `corporatism`; an absent subtype list now means that no reviewed child-school list is being asserted.

These are display and explainer changes only. The result-distance calculation is unchanged.

## Question wording corrections

Two prompts received neutral, same-construct wording corrections:

- `q0318`: “austerity imposed by bureaucracy” became “mandatory consumption limits administered by public agencies.”
- `q0380`: “safety bureaucracies” became a specific claim about regulatory agencies, barriers to entry, and incumbent firms.

Tests guard that this review layer changes the prompt and review metadata without changing the item’s layer, domain, theory context, or axis weights.

## Replacement-required question debt

The prior ten replacement-required items are quarantined from public scoring and research forms: `q0002`, `q0078`, `q0103`, `q0109`, `q0143`, `q0196`, `q0277`, `q0409`, `sq11`, and `sq12`.

Six more items now carry explicit replacement proposals:

- `q0211` bundles legal status, labor access, education, and housing into one side of a causal comparison.
- `q0238` combines opposition to cultural homogenization with opposition to durable group-specific legal protections.
- `q0297` states a general caution about group data but does not cleanly identify the listed redistribution, regulation, or centralization construct.
- `q0298` combines permission for cultural difference with rejection of unequal legal status.
- `q0306` asks respondents to endorse human flourishing and ecological limits simultaneously, so agreement does not discriminate the stated human–nature priority direction.
- `q0346` bundles voice, exit, rights, expertise, and competition into an inclusive legitimacy claim rather than isolating one directional judgment.

All sixteen proposals remain outside the active bank. The corresponding flawed items are now marked `needs-rewrite` and inactive, even where this reduces synthetic coverage. They should receive expert review and cognitive interviews before new replacement IDs enter scoring; coverage guards must not override known content defects.

## Redundancy to resolve with respondent evidence

- `q0048` / `q0410` substantially overlap on the descriptive local-information problem in central economic planning.
- `q0242` / `q0406` substantially overlap on whether coercive civil law should be justified through reasons accessible outside one religious tradition.
- `q0238` / `q0298` substantially overlap on equal legal status across cultural groups while each bundles that principle with a second pluralism claim.

All three pairs remain active. They should be retained only as an intentional reliability or parallel-form design, or reduced after cognitive interviewing and pilot item statistics identify the clearer item. Editorial similarity alone is not enough to choose the survivor.

## Review references

- [Pew Research Center: Writing Survey Questions](https://www.pewresearch.org/writing-survey-questions/) recommends clear, specific wording, warns against double-barreled items, and emphasizes pretesting.
- [CDC: Survey Development Guidance](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/136522/cdc_136522_DS1.pdf) likewise recommends separating multiple concepts and avoiding leading wording.
- [CDC CCQDER](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/CCQDER/index.html) describes cognitive interviewing and question evaluation for determining how respondents understand and answer items.
- [RAND: The Emergence of Noopolitik](https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/monograph_reports/MR1033/RAND_MR1033.pdf) locates the early cyberocracy concept in information-age governance research.
- [Syntheism](https://syntheism.com/) describes Syntheism in process-philosophical and process-religious terms.
- [Cambridge University Press: The Saffronization of Public Knowledge in India](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/public-humanities/article/saffronization-of-public-knowledge-in-india/520D2B38E5C37E657CB2657D4D608322) uses saffronization for a politicizing process associated with Hindutva.
- [Encyclopedia.com: Labor Zionism](https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/labor-zionism) and [Zionist Revisionist Movement](https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/zionist-revisionist-movement) document distinct currents within Zionism.
- [Journal of Democracy: Islamist Parties and Democracy — Three Kinds of Movements](https://www.journalofdemocracy.org/articles/islamist-parties-and-democracy-three-kinds-of-movements/) supports distinguishing democratic participation within Islamic political frameworks from undifferentiated Islamism.
- [Journal of Democracy: The Rise of “Muslim Democracy”](https://www.journalofdemocracy.org/articles/the-rise-of-muslim-democracy/) treats Muslim Democracy as a pragmatic electoral development rather than a subtype of one constitutional theory.
- [Swiss Political Science Review: Clarifying and Defining the Concept of Liquid Democracy](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/spsr.12486) treats proxy and delegative terminology as names used for the underlying decision system, not child ideologies.
- [Journal of Democracy: Delegative Democracy](https://www.journalofdemocracy.org/articles/delegative-democracy/) documents the separate comparative-politics use of “delegative democracy” for weakly constrained executive rule.
- [Global Society: Global Governance or World Federalism?](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/13600820600816282) distinguishes alternative cosmopolitan institutional models rather than nesting global governance under world federalism.
- [International Journal of Constitutional Law: Constitutional Patriotism — An Introduction](https://academic.oup.com/icon/article/6/1/67/669061) distinguishes constitutional patriotism from nationalism.
- [Journal of Political Ideologies: A Space for Freedom — The Paleolibertarian Coalition](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/13569317.2023.2296403) describes paleoconservatives and radical libertarians as distinct coalition partners rather than a parent-child taxonomy.
