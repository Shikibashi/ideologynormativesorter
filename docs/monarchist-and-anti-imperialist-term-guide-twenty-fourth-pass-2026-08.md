# Monarchist-and-anti-imperialist term-guide twenty-fourth pass

Date: 2026-08-11

## Scope

This pass reviewed the three labels that still lacked stable direct term
guides: Constitutional Monarchism, Anti-Imperialism, and Traditional
Monarchist. It adds direct term guides, layer summaries, and regression tests.
It does not change centroids, axis weights, question prompts, question
selection, result eligibility, or scoring behavior.

## Direct term guides

- **Constitutional Monarchism** now identifies a hereditary monarch serving as
  head of state within constitutional limits. Parliamentary versions can make
  the role largely ceremonial, while executive constitutional monarchies retain
  constrained governing authority. It is a regime form, not traditional or
  absolute monarchy by definition.
- **Anti-Imperialism** now identifies opposition to colonial rule, external
  domination, and unequal dependence together with support for subordinated
  peoples’ self-determination. It is a cross-cutting orientation that can be
  hosted by socialist, nationalist, liberal, non-aligned, or other politics.
- **Traditional Monarchist** now identifies a conservative royalist orientation
  that treats hereditary monarchy as historically or traditionally legitimate
  and often favors meaningful royal prerogative. It is distinct from both the
  limited head-of-state model of constitutional monarchism and the stronger
  indivisible-sovereignty claim associated with absolute monarchism.

Each guide is keyed to the stable catalog id and shown alone, so a specific
result is not displaced by a generic parent-family definition.

## Layer summaries

All three labels now have explicit normative, descriptive, and prescriptive
summaries:

- Constitutional Monarchism treats hereditary continuity or a nonpartisan head
  of state as compatible with constitutional and democratic government; its
  institutional variants differ over the crown’s remaining authority.
- Anti-Imperialism treats political equality and self-determination as opposed
  to colonial or imperial domination, explains dependence through multiple
  mechanisms, and favors decolonization, sovereignty, solidarity, and sometimes
  non-alignment without prescribing one economic system.
- Traditional Monarchist treats dynastic continuity, inherited authority,
  hierarchy, and historical or religious legitimacy as political goods while
  leaving room for disagreement over constitutional limits and representation.
  Its guide does not infer absolute monarchy or one religious doctrine.

These distinctions separate a constitutional regime design, a cross-cutting
anti-domination orientation, and a conservative royalist position. They are
editorial descriptions of the catalog profiles, not claims that every movement
using one of these names has the same complete program.

## Source basis

- Lisa James and Robert Hazell, “What is constitutional monarchy and what is
  its role in the UK?”, UCL Constitution Unit, on the distinction between a
  monarch as head of state and elected government, constitutional conventions,
  and ceremonial or formal royal roles:
  https://www.ucl.ac.uk/social-historical-sciences/constitution-unit/constitution-unit-publications/constitution-unit-briefings/what-constitutional-monarchy-and-what-its-role-uk
- Robert Hazell and Bob Morris, *The Monarchy and the Constitution*, Oxford
  University Press, on constitutional monarchy, royal prerogative, succession,
  and the separation of head-of-state and head-of-government roles:
  https://academic.oup.com/book/6972
- Stefanie Michels Hilfrich, “Anti-imperialism and anti-colonialism,” *The
  Wiley Blackwell Encyclopedia of Social and Political Movements*, on the
  broader and changing scope of anti-imperialism beyond opposition to formal
  colonial rule alone:
  https://doi.org/10.1002/9781118455074.wbeoe120
- Elizabeth Anderson, “Empire, Popular Sovereignty, and the Problem of
  Self-and-Other-Determination,” *Perspectives on Politics*, on imperial
  domination, popular sovereignty, and the political problem of self-
  determination:
  https://doi.org/10.1017/S1537592721000020
- Michael Herb, “Monarchism as an Authoritarian Institution,” *The Oxford
  Handbook of Authoritarian Politics*, on monarchism as hereditary succession
  and as an institution whose political authority can vary rather than one
  fixed regime type:
  https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordhb/9780198871996.013.63
- “Conservatism,” *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, on living tradition,
  inherited institutions, authority, hierarchy, and the distinction between
  cautious conservatism and dogmatic reaction:
  https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2022/entries/conservatism/

The catalog’s decision to treat these as separate result profiles is an
editorial taxonomy choice informed by the sources. The sources do not imply
one universally settled classification for every constitutional monarchist,
anti-imperialist, or traditional monarchist movement.
