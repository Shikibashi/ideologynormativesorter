## Scope

This pass addresses the five remaining scored labels that had source coverage
but no bespoke usage or boundary note: Social Liberalism, Distributism, Welfare
Chauvinism, Anti-Imperialism, and Regionalism. It adds claim-matched source
records and neighboring-term distinctions. No centroid, axis weight, role,
question prompt, or result eligibility changed.

## Editorial decisions

- **Social Liberalism** now identifies the positive-liberty and social-condition
  strand of liberalism while leaving the scale of redistribution, welfare, and
  market regulation open. It is not automatically Social Democracy or a label
  for progressive cultural politics.
- **Distributism** now centers the program on widely distributed productive
  ownership. Family firms, guilds, cooperatives, and anti-monopoly measures are
  associated proposals, not interchangeable definitions; the label is not
  simply small-business advocacy or agrarian nostalgia.
- **Welfare Chauvinism** now uses the research meaning of restricting immigrant
  access to welfare for a national or perceived native in-group, including both
  conditional and strict forms. It is distinct from general anti-immigration
  views, small-government views, and welfare ethnocentrism toward citizen
  minorities.
- **Anti-Imperialism** now presents a family of anti-colonial and anti-dominance
  projects that can differ over independence, non-alignment, internationalism,
  socialism, and decolonial reconstruction. Shared anti-imperial language does
  not determine domestic institutions or economic policy.
- **Regionalism** now distinguishes territorial autonomy movements, regional
  administration, and decentralization. Regionalism can seek devolution within
  a unitary state and is not automatically federalism or secession.

These sources explain the labels and their boundaries; they do not validate
numeric centroids, infer respondent identity, or establish psychometric
performance.
