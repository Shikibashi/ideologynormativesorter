# Editorial twelfth pass: intergroup-contact evidence scope

Version: `2026-08-editorial-v12`
Date: 2026-08-12

This pass corrects `q0207`, a descriptive item about intergroup contact. The
previous prompt said that effects were weaker for ethnic, racial, religious,
and immigrant targets. The cited review supports substantial heterogeneity and
weaker effects for ethnic or racial prejudice, but it does not support treating
all four target categories as a single uniformly weaker group. The revised item
keeps the review's qualified positive result, names the random-assignment and
delayed-outcome scope, and states the adult racial/ethnic evidence gap.

| Item | Change | Research basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `q0207` | Rewrites the prompt and evidence note around the 27 randomized experiments with outcomes measured at least one day later; removes the unsupported all-target “weaker” comparison. | [Paluck, Green, and Green, “The Contact Hypothesis Re-evaluated”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioural-public-policy/article/contact-hypothesis-reevaluated/142C913E7FA9E121277B29E994124EC5) and [Pettigrew and Tropp, “A Meta-Analytic Test of Intergroup Contact Theory”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16737372/) |

The change preserves the descriptive layer and cultural-plasticity mapping. It is
an evidence-scope correction, not a claim that the literature establishes a
universal policy effect or that the quiz's psychometric performance has been
validated.
