# Identity, Nationalism, Multiculturalism, and Decolonial Sovereignty

## Purpose

This investigation asks whether the sorter currently distinguishes several traditions that use the language of identity, peoplehood, nationhood, autonomy, or sovereignty but make substantively different political claims.

The central finding is that **identity language is not itself a sufficient ideology discriminator**. Majority ethnonationalism, minority national self-determination, Black community nationalism, Black separatism, Indigenous sovereignty, multicultural accommodation, and Pan-African transnational solidarity can overlap in vocabulary while differing sharply on membership, domination, territory, political authority, institutional strategy, and the scale of solidarity.

The production taxonomy should therefore avoid group-name substitution. In particular, the sorter should never derive Black nationalism, Indigenous sovereignty, or other minority self-determination traditions by cloning the Ethnonationalist centroid and changing the named people.

## Current production roles

The current taxonomy remains unchanged by this investigation:

- **Ethnonationalism** — primary.
- **Multiculturalism** — modifier.
- **Indigenism** — specialist.
- **Black Nationalism** — not yet a production label.
- **Pan-Africanism** — not yet a production label.

The new specialist module is quarantined measurement. No role changes should occur until respondent-grounded validation supports them.

## 1. Ethnonationalism

The existing Ethnonationalist label is useful if it is kept narrow: it should describe a political program in which nationhood is substantially ascriptive or inherited and where the state is expected to protect the continuity or political predominance of a historic core nation.

This is not the same question as whether a minority people has a claim to self-determination. The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy treats nationalism as involving both conceptions of national membership and claims to self-determination, and explicitly notes that self-determination need not mean full independent statehood.

### Measurement implication

Ethnonationalism should be measured with at least two independent local distinctions:

1. **Ascriptive membership** — ancestry/inherited culture versus civic or adoptive membership.
2. **Dominant-nation congruence** — whether the state may privilege preservation of the historic core nation's demographic/cultural predominance.

A minority autonomy claim should not score as ethnonationalist merely because the claimant describes a culturally distinct people.

## 2. Multiculturalism

The current decision to treat Multiculturalism as a modifier remains plausible, but it should not be reduced to generic tolerance or cosmopolitanism.

Liberal multiculturalist theory includes recognition and positive accommodation of group difference. The literature distinguishes multiple institutional claims, including exemptions, language and cultural supports, representation, and self-government rights. Kymlicka's framework especially distinguishes immigrant/polyethnic accommodation from self-government claims by national minorities and Indigenous peoples.

That makes multiculturalism cross-cutting rather than obviously a self-contained primary ideology. A liberal, social democrat, conservative, Indigenous nationalist, or other respondent may endorse some multicultural accommodations for different reasons.

### Measurement implication

Keep **pluralist accommodation** as a local construct. Do not infer it solely from open borders, universalism, or low traditionalism.

Role review after real data should ask:

- Does multiculturalist self-identification predict a stable response pattern beyond broader liberal/social-democratic families?
- Does the construct behave as a modifier across several primary traditions?
- Are self-government rights empirically separable from immigrant/religious/cultural accommodation?

Until then, modifier remains the conservative taxonomy choice.

## 3. Black Nationalism

Black Nationalism should not be modeled as a race-specific form of the existing Ethnonationalist label.

Robert A. Brown and Todd C. Shaw's empirical work found two attitudinal dimensions within Black Nationalism: **community nationalism** and **separatist nationalism**. Andrew Valls similarly distinguishes the classical claim for an independent state from post-civil-rights community nationalism, which seeks more limited forms of Black autonomy and has important similarities to minority-national self-determination.

This gives the sorter a better architecture than generating several micro-labels.

### Recommended taxonomy

Use one candidate specialist family:

**Black Nationalism**

with measured variants:

- **Community nationalism** — institution-building, self-reliance, community control, collective autonomy, without requiring territorial separation.
- **Separatist nationalism** — stronger preference for territorial separation, independent political authority, or statehood.

Do not create separate production labels for these variants unless respondent data later demonstrates that users reliably occupy distinct stable clusters and understand the labels.

### Measurement implication

The key constructs are:

- community autonomy;
- minority self-government;
- territorial separatism.

Race alone is not a construct.

## 4. Pan-Africanism

Pan-Africanism should be measured separately from Black separatism and separately from generic ethnonationalism.

Spence, Shaw, and Brown explicitly investigate and distinguish attitudinal support for Pan-Africanism from Black separatism. Rita Abrahamsen's morphological analysis also shows that Pan-Africanism itself contains multiple visions: internationalist/transnational solidarity, national-sovereigntist, and nativist/racially bounded variants.

This means Pan-Africanism cannot safely be represented as either:

- a synonym for Black Nationalism; or
- a single high-score point on an ethnic-boundary axis.

### Recommended taxonomy

For now, treat **Pan-Africanism** as a candidate role review with a local **Pan-African solidarity** construct. It may eventually be a specialist tradition, modifier, or family-level orientation depending on respondent data.

The first validation target should be the transnational solidarity/unity component because it is clearly distinguishable from territorial Black separatism. Later work can test whether sovereigntist and nativist Pan-African variants deserve subtype modeling.

A respondent should be able to score highly on both Black community nationalism and Pan-Africanism. The scoring model therefore uses independent affinities rather than exclusive nearest-label classification.

## 5. Indigenous sovereignty and decolonization

The current `indigenism` label is too compressed conceptually, even though it correctly recognizes Indigenous sovereignty, anti-colonialism, culture, land, and communal governance.

Contemporary Indigenous political theory treats sovereignty and decolonization as more than an ethnic-national membership question. David Myer Temin's *Remapping Sovereignty* reconstructs Indigenous activist-theorists as reworking the relation between sovereignty, self-determination, settler colonialism, and political order. The contemporary literature also stresses that settler colonialism can be an ongoing institutional relation, especially through dispossession and control of land, rather than merely a historical event.

The SEP Colonialism entry summarizes a further distinction between politics of recognition/reconciliation and Indigenous resurgence or refusal associated with thinkers such as Glen Coulthard and Leanne Betasamosake Simpson.

### Recommended taxonomy

Keep one specialist family initially, but consider renaming the user-facing label after review:

**Indigenous Sovereignty / Resurgence**

rather than a generic `Indigenism` if that wording proves clearer and less flattening.

Measure at least two variants inside the family:

- **Institutional self-government** — treaties, recognized jurisdiction, negotiated self-government, restoration of authority within or alongside existing states.
- **Resurgence and refusal** — rebuilding Indigenous legal/political institutions on their own terms, emphasizing continuing settler-colonial domination and land-based authority without making state recognition the conceptual starting point.

### Measurement implication

The relevant constructs are:

- minority/collective self-government;
- decolonial and land-based sovereignty;
- recognition versus refusal/resurgence.

Do not treat `decolonial sovereignty` as a new scored ideology by itself. It is better treated as a construct or orientation until there is evidence for a self-contained tradition that the questionnaire can distinguish.

## 6. Why the module is multi-affinity

These traditions are not mutually exclusive alternatives.

Examples of coherent combinations include:

- Black community nationalism + Pan-Africanism;
- Indigenous self-government + multicultural accommodation;
- minority nationalism + liberal constitutionalism;
- multiculturalism + social democracy.

A nearest-single-centroid classifier would erase this structure. The specialist module therefore calculates local constructs and then independent tradition affinities. Multiple traditions may fit strongly at once.

For traditions with internal variants, such as Black Nationalism and Indigenous sovereignty, the module retains the best-fitting variant under one family ID rather than multiplying display labels.

## 7. Guardrails against false symmetry and meme taxonomy

1. **No group-name swapping.** Do not create `X nationalism` by copying the Ethnonationalist centroid and replacing the ethnic group.
2. **No automatic moral symmetry assumption.** The measurement model should describe political claims and historical functions accurately; it should not assume that majority exclusionary nationalism and minority anti-domination/self-determination projects are conceptually identical because both invoke group identity.
3. **No automatic moral asymmetry either.** Historical context does not determine a respondent's answers. Items should measure membership, hierarchy, autonomy, territory, and strategy directly.
4. **No subtype explosion.** Community/separatist Black nationalism and recognition/resurgence Indigenous politics remain variants until real data supports separate user-facing labels.
5. **No generic `decolonialism` result.** Anti-colonial or decolonial commitments can modify many ideologies and must earn any independent role through measurement.
6. **No racial essentialism in items.** Questions target institutional and political claims, not assertions about inherent racial traits.

## 8. Validation plan before promotion

Before Black Nationalism, Pan-Africanism, or any revised Indigenous label becomes a production result:

1. expose the specialist module through an opt-in/depth UI;
2. collect criterion self-identification independently of the module result;
3. test internal reliability and local construct structure;
4. inspect item discrimination and differential item functioning where sample size permits;
5. build confusion matrices against Ethnonationalism, Civic Nationalism, Multiculturalism, Indigenism, and nearby primary families;
6. test whether community vs separatist and recognition vs resurgence variants reproduce in respondent data;
7. check whether Pan-Africanism behaves as a specialist ideology, a cross-cutting modifier, or a family-level orientation;
8. only then change catalog roles or add stable production IDs.
