# Question-context twenty-first pass

This pass uses `2026-08-question-context-v26` and adds bespoke context/source
records to 46 active questions across environmental ethics and policy,
foreign policy and war, democracy and constitutionalism, technology and AI,
and political strategy. It clarifies construct and layer boundaries without
changing prompts, response scales, axis weights, or label assignments.

## Scope

- Environment and climate: `q0301`, `q0304`, `q0305`, `q0313`, `q0315`–`q0317`, `q0319`, and `q0418`.
- Foreign policy and war: `q0321`, `q0324`–`q0326`, `q0334`, `q0336`–`q0338`, `q0401`, and `q0403`.
- Democracy, expertise, and constitutionalism: `q0341`–`q0345`, `q0356`, `q0357`, `q0359`, and the confidence-coverage items `q0474`–`q0477`.
- Technology, AI, and surveillance: `q0361`–`q0365`, `q0374`, `q0377`, and `q0379`.
- Strategy and political change: `q0381`–`q0385`, `q0394`, and `q0399`.

The notes keep descriptive claims about observed mechanisms separate from
normative claims about standing or legitimacy and prescriptive claims about
institutional design. For example, intergenerational concern does not choose
a climate instrument; a defensive rationale does not authorize every
intervention; technical expertise does not settle who should decide; privacy
does not require that every investigation be impossible; and revolutionary
ends do not settle the permissibility of coercive means.

## Source basis

The records use the existing source catalog, including the [SEP entry on
Environmental Ethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-environmental/),
the [IPCC Sixth Assessment Synthesis
Report](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/syr/downloads/report/IPCC_AR6_SYR_FullVolume.pdf),
the [SEP entry on War](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/war/), the [UN
Charter](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter/full-text), [International
IDEA’s Emergency Powers guidance](https://www.idea.int/publications/catalogue/emergency-powers),
the [SEP entry on Democracy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/democracy/index.html),
the [OECD guidance on evidence for good
governance](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/mobilising-evidence-for-good-governance_3f6f736b-en/full-report/component-5.html),
the [SEP entry on the Ethics of AI and
Robotics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-ai/), [NIST’s AI Risk
Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework),
and the [SEP entries on Civil
Disobedience](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2025/entries/civil-disobedience/)
and [Revolution](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/revolution/index.html).

These sources support definitions, institutional boundaries, and cautious
framing; they do not establish psychometric validity or determine a
respondent’s answer.
